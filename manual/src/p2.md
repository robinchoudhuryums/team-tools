## §2-2 Ordering rules for this department

### §2-2.1 Delivery radius and OOR

Every order has to fall inside the delivery radius for the item ordered, and **the radius varies by item**.

When an order can't be completed because the address falls outside the radius for that item, it is notated **OOR — Out of Range**.

> **Policy — walkers on an insurance order are not shipped out of state.** For a walker processed through insurance, out of state **is** out of range: the order is OOR and cannot be completed. An **out-of-pocket** order lifts the state limit — but never a delivery radius.

For other items the radius is a distance, not a state line, so check the radius for the specific item rather than assuming from the address. **Every item's area — its states, cities or radius, and what lifts out of pocket — is in Reference → OOP price &amp; area eligibility**, the app's lookup of the out-of-pocket pricing sheet. Medicaid is also state-specific and does not travel with the patient — see [[§10-15]].

### §2-2.2 HCPCS codes in this department

A HCPCS code names a **category** of equipment, not a model, so one code can refer to different models in the same category. Confirm which product is actually on the order rather than inferring from the code.

- **Manual wheelchairs** are coded by type and weight capacity, K0001 to K0007 — see [[§2-4.1]]. A 20" seat on a standard-weight chair can appear as **K0003 with E2201** — E2201 is an add-on code for a 20–24" seat, not a modifier.
- **Hospital beds** carry one code with a mattress and another without: the semi-electric bed is **E0260** with a mattress and **E0261** without.

These codes are each shared by two of our products, so a code lookup returns more than one:

| HCPCS | Items |
|---|---|
| E0143 | Front-wheeled walker **and** basic steel rollator |
| E0149 | Bariatric walker **and** heavy duty steel rollator |
| E0265 | Fully-electric **and** Hi-Lo hospital beds |
| E0635 | Medline battery-powered lift (MDS450EL) **and** heavy duty battery lift (MDS700EL) |

### §2-2.3 Product capacity vs. coverage threshold

These are different numbers and shouldn't be used interchangeably:

- **Product capacity** — what the equipment is rated to hold
- **Coverage threshold** — the patient weight above which insurance will cover the heavier-duty item

A standard walker is rated to 300 lbs; the bariatric coverage threshold is a patient weight **over 300 lbs**. A bariatric walker is rated to 500 lbs, which is not the threshold.

> **Note — below the threshold.** Bariatric equipment can still be provided to a patient who doesn't meet the weight threshold, if the patient pays the difference. The amounts are on the Upgrade Fee Sheet (Mobility) — see [[§2-12]].

## §2-4 Manual wheelchairs

{{eq:manual-wheelchair|allowance}}

> **Note** — Product Wt. is the weight of the chair. User Capacity is what it will hold. The Guardian Reclining chair's weight is not published by the manufacturer.

### §2-4.1 Coverage documentation

Medicare defines each manual wheelchair code partly by weight capacity, and covers the heavier-duty codes by the **patient's** weight:

| Code | Type | Weight capacity (code definition) | Covered when the patient… |
|---|---|---|---|
| K0001 · K0002 · K0003 | Standard, hemi, lightweight | 250 lbs or less | Meets the standard criteria |
| K0004 | High-strength lightweight | Not defined by capacity | Meets the high-strength criteria |
| **K0006** | Heavy duty | Over 250 lbs | Weighs **over 250 lbs**, or has severe spasticity |
| **K0007** | Extra heavy duty | Over 300 lbs | Weighs **over 300 lbs** |

> **Note — manufacturer ratings can be higher than the code allows.** Medline lists some of its standard chairs at 300 lbs, but a K0001, K0002 or K0003 chair is defined by Medicare as 250 lbs or less. **Use the capacities in this manual**, which follow the Medicare code definitions, rather than the manufacturer's website.

> **Watch-out — the extra-wide chairs are K0007.** Both the Guardian Extra Wide and the Shuttle Extra-Wide are K0007, which Medicare covers only for a patient **over 300 lbs**. A patient between 251 and 300 lbs qualifies for **K0006** — the Medline K3 Guardian 20" — not an extra-wide one.

### §2-4.2 Size changes

There's no cutoff for a seat width or depth change. What happens depends on where the order is:

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Before delivery** | The caller can ask for a different size. Email the Manual Mobility Q rather than transferring — the team acts on it, depending on the circumstance. See [[§0-12.3]] |
| **After delivery** | Still possible, through Service or a swap on a pick-up ticket, depending on the circumstance — see [[§6-7]] |

For example, a patient whose provider re-measures them before delivery can have the order changed by the Manual Mobility team; a patient who finds the delivered chair too narrow goes to Service, or the chair is swapped on a pick-up ticket, depending on the circumstance.

### §2-5.2 Rollators

Rollators include a seat, basket, and hand brakes. The basic model comes in blue, burgundy, and light blue.

{{eq:rollator|seat,allowance,+Seat Height}}

> **Policy — why a rollator carries an upgrade fee.** A rollator bills under the same HCPCS code as a walker (E0143 or E0149), so insurance pays for the walker, and the seat and brakes are an upgrade beyond medical necessity. The patient pays the difference as an upgrade fee unless one of the waiver rules applies, and must agree before the order proceeds. The fees and the waiver rules are in [[§2-10]].

## §2-10 Upgrade fees

Two schedules apply depending on where in the process the upgrade is requested. **Both are published here because they apply to different situations** — confirm which applies before quoting.

{{fees}}

### §2-10.1 When a fee is waived

There are exactly **three** waiver rules, and if a situation is not one of them the fee is charged: any active Medicaid plan waives the rollator fees; full Medicaid in a state where UMS is enrolled, or BCBS commercial as primary, waives the bed fees; patient lift fees are never waived. The rules, the matrix and the traps are in [[§10-16.1]].

## §2-11 FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How much does a rollator weigh? | The basic steel rollator is 19 lbs; the heavy duty is 24 lbs. See [[§2-5.2]]. |
| What are the hospital bed dimensions? | 80" L × 36" W × 15–20" H, adjustable by crank. The mattress is 80" × 36" × 6". |
| When does a patient qualify for a bariatric walker? | At a patient weight over 300 lbs. Note that this is the coverage threshold, not the product's 500 lb capacity. See [[§2-2.3]]. |
| Does Medicare cover bathroom items? | It depends on the item. The 3-in-1 commode can be covered by Medicare. Shower chairs and tub transfer benches are TX Medicaid only. Raised toilet seats are out-of-pocket only. See [[§2-8.3]]. |
| Can bed rails be changed after delivery? | Yes on the semi-electric bed — half-length and full-length can be swapped. No on the bariatric bed. |
| Can a patient change wheelchair size after ordering? | Yes. Before delivery the Manual Mobility team can change it; after delivery it's Service, or a swap on a pick-up ticket. See [[§2-4.2]]. |
| Why can't we ship this walker? | On an insurance order a walker isn't shipped out of state — it's notated OOR. Out of pocket, only the item's delivery radius applies. See [[§2-2.1]]. |
| Is there an arm blood pressure cuff? | No. Only the wrist monitor is available. |
