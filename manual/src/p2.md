## §2-2 Ordering rules for this department

### §2-2.1 Delivery radius and OOR

Every order has to fall inside the delivery radius for the item ordered, and **the radius varies by item**. Walkers have a narrower radius than most equipment.

When an order can't be completed because the address falls outside the radius for that item, it is notated **OOR — Out of Range**.

> **Policy — walkers are not shipped out of state.** For walkers specifically, out of state **is** out of range. Any out-of-state walker order is OOR and cannot be completed.

For other items the radius is a distance, not a state line, so check the radius for the specific item rather than assuming from the address. **Every item's radius is in the Delivery Radius list** — see [[§2-12]]. Medicaid is also state-specific and does not travel with the patient — see [[§10-15]].

### §2-2.2 Shared HCPCS codes

Two pairs of items bill under the same code, so a code lookup returns more than one product:

| HCPCS | Items |
|---|---|
| E0143 | Front-wheeled walker **and** basic steel rollator |
| E0149 | Bariatric walker **and** heavy duty steel rollator |

Confirm which product is actually on the order rather than inferring from the code.

### §2-2.3 Product capacity vs. coverage threshold

These are different numbers and shouldn't be used interchangeably:

- **Product capacity** — what the equipment is rated to hold
- **Coverage threshold** — the patient weight above which insurance will cover the heavier-duty item

A standard walker is rated to 300 lbs; the bariatric coverage threshold is a patient weight **over 300 lbs**. A bariatric walker is rated to 500 lbs, which is not the threshold.

## §2-4 Manual wheelchairs

{{eq:manual-wheelchair|allowance}}

> **Note** — Product Wt. is the weight of the chair. User Capacity is what it will hold. The Guardian Reclining chair's weight is not published by the manufacturer.

### §2-4.1 Coverage documentation

Medicare defines each manual wheelchair code partly by weight capacity, and covers the heavier-duty codes by the **patient's** weight:

| Code | Type | Weight capacity (code definition) | Covered when the patient… |
|---|---|---|---|
| K0001 · K0002 · K0003 | Standard, hemi, lightweight | 250 lbs or less | Meets the standard criteria |
| K0004 | High-strength lightweight | Not defined by capacity | Meets the high-strength criteria |
| **K0006** | Heavy duty | Over 250 lbs | Weighs **over 250 lbs**, or has severe spasticity |
| **K0007** | Extra heavy duty | Over 300 lbs | Weighs **over 300 lbs** |

> **Note — manufacturer ratings can be higher than the code allows.** Medline lists some of its standard chairs at 300 lbs, but a K0001, K0002 or K0003 chair is defined by Medicare as 250 lbs or less. **Use the capacities in this manual**, which follow the Medicare code definitions, rather than the manufacturer's website.

> **Watch-out — the extra-wide chairs are K0007.** Both the Guardian Extra Wide and the Shuttle Extra-Wide are K0007, which Medicare covers only for a patient **over 300 lbs**. A patient between 251 and 300 lbs qualifies for **K0006** — the Medline K3 Guardian 20" — not an extra-wide one.

### §2-4.2 Size changes

Seat width and depth can be changed before the order is validated. After validation the order has to be reopened, which requires review — email the Manual Mobility Q rather than transferring. See [[§0-12.3]].

### §2-6.1 Knee walkers

Knee walkers are an out-of-pocket item, with one exception.

> **Note — VGM Homelink.** Homelink is a national managed care and ancillary healthcare coordination company. It connects insurance payers and case managers with a network of independent healthcare providers, and UMS is one of those providers. A patient whose benefits are coordinated through Homelink may have the knee walker covered rather than paying out of pocket. If a caller mentions Homelink or a case manager, check before quoting an out-of-pocket price.

## §2-10 Upgrade fees

Two schedules apply depending on where in the process the upgrade is requested. **Both are published here because they apply to different situations** — confirm which applies before quoting.

{{fees}}

### §2-10.1 Waiver matrix — the only three rules

There are exactly **three** waiver rules. If a situation is not one of them, the fee is charged.

{{diagram:waivers}}

| Rule | Trigger | Waives |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Any active Medicaid plan, any state, primary or secondary — **regardless of whether UMS is enrolled in that state** | Standard **and** heavy-duty rollator fees |
| **B** | Full Medicaid or a Medicaid MCO **in a state where UMS is enrolled**. Medicaid is billed primary for the bed | Fully-electric and Hi-Lo bed fees |
| **C** | **BCBS commercial** is the patient's primary | Fully-electric and Hi-Lo bed fees |

{{waiver-matrix}}

> **Note — uncommon payers.** Workers' Compensation or the VA, for example, may pay for the item in full, including the upgrade fee. See [[§10-16]].

> **Policy — three traps in this matrix.**
>
> 1. **QMB-only is not full Medicaid.** It waives the standard rollator fee only. The heavy-duty rollator fee is still charged, and QMB-only does not qualify for Rule B.
> 2. **Enrollment matters for beds, not rollators.** Rule A applies whether or not UMS is enrolled in the patient's Medicaid state. Rule B requires enrollment.
> 3. **BCBS waives bed fees, not the rollator fee.** This corrects earlier guidance that said otherwise.
>
> Rule B includes Rule A. Patient lift fees are never waived. CSRs cannot waive or reduce a fee outside these three rules. The authoritative version is [[§10-16]].

> **Script — patient has Medicaid and wants a rollator.** "You have an active Medicaid plan, so there's no upgrade cost for the rollator. Let me get that confirmed on your order."

> **Script — patient has out-of-state Medicaid and wants a bed upgrade.** There's no published list of enrolled states, so don't commit:
>
> "I'll need to have that verified — whether the upgrade cost applies depends on your specific Medicaid plan. Let me check and get back to you before anything is finalized."

> **Watch-out** — The bariatric bed and the bariatric wheelchair have no upgrade fee. They are separate items requiring their own medical necessity documentation, not upgrades.

## §2-11 FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How much does a rollator weigh? | The basic steel rollator is 19 lbs; the heavy duty is 24 lbs. See [[§2-5.2]]. |
| What are the hospital bed dimensions? | 80" L × 36" W × 15–20" H, adjustable by crank. The mattress is 80" × 36" × 6". |
| When does a patient qualify for a bariatric walker? | At a patient weight over 300 lbs. Note that this is the coverage threshold, not the product's 500 lb capacity. See [[§2-2.3]]. |
| Does Medicare cover bathroom items? | It depends on the item. The 3-in-1 commode can be covered by Medicare. Shower chairs and tub transfer benches are TX Medicaid only. Raised toilet seats are out-of-pocket only. See [[§2-8.3]]. |
| Can bed rails be changed after delivery? | Yes on the semi-electric bed — half-length and full-length can be swapped. No on the bariatric bed. |
| Can a patient change wheelchair size after ordering? | Before validation, yes. After validation the order must be reopened. See [[§2-4.2]]. |
| Why can't we ship this walker? | Check the delivery radius for the item. Walkers have a narrower radius than most equipment, and out-of-range orders are notated OOR. See [[§2-2.1]]. |
| Is there an arm blood pressure cuff? | No. Only the wrist monitor is available. |
