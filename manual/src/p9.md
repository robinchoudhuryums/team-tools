## §9-1 Scope

> **Policy — Sales is predominantly for PMD orders.** There are additional sub-teams in Sales that route separately:

| Area | Queue | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Power mobility | Sales Q | The main Sales function |
| PAP | **PAP Q** | Recent effort to generate new PAP orders. Technically part of Sales, but routed to its own queue |
| Manual wheelchairs | **Sales MWC Q** | Manual wheelchair sales leads |

The **Sales Eligibility team is also a division of Sales** — see [[§4-2]]. That's where an order sits between a lead being qualified and it reaching the Power department.

## §9-2 The Sales process

Sales is primarily **outbound**: calling back leads already in the system — people who expressed interest in a power mobility device.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Lead outreach | The Sales team calls back leads in the system who expressed interest in a PMD |
| 2. Confirm the lead | Discuss with the customer and confirm they want to pursue a PMD **with UMS** |
| 3. Collect details | Basic patient details, **insurance details**, and **PCP information** |
| 4. **Create the transaction** | A TRX is created in the system, after double-checking there is no existing patient account. This is where the order officially starts |
| 5. Schedule the Mobility Evaluation | An appointment with the PCP or another qualifying provider, specifically for a **"Mobility Evaluation for Power Mobility Device"** |
| 6. **Verify the appointment** | Date, time and provider confirmed with **both the patient and the provider's office**. Only then is it verified |
| 7. Eligibility check | The Sales Eligibility team confirms we can work with the patient's insurance. Reference: the Payor/HCPCS Order Acceptance Sheet, for PMD and scooter acceptance by region |
| 8. **Handoff to Power** | The order moves to the Power intake team and leaves Sales' purview |

> **Watch-out — the appointment must be for a PMD evaluation specifically.** A general appointment with the PCP is not a Mobility Evaluation. This is the same problem that surfaces later in Power when an appointment turns out not to have been for a PMD evaluation — see [[§4-3.3]].

> **Note — "verified" means both sides.** An appointment date on the order is not the same as a verified appointment. It has to be confirmed with the patient **and** the provider's office.

Once the order reaches Power, the process in [[§4-1.1]] takes over.

### §9-2.1 The Qualified Lead path

If the customer has **already had a Mobility Evaluation for a PMD** with their provider and the MDO sends us the qualifying visit notes, the order fast-tracks.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | As above — confirm the lead, collect details, create the TRX |
| 5. Notes received | The MDO sends the qualifying visit notes. They are acceptable if they mention the appointment was to **discuss a PMD for the patient** |
| 6. Two verifications | The insurance is verified **and** the visit notes are verified as acceptable |
| 7. **Handoff, skipping PAK** | The order goes directly to the **Qualifications** sub-team in Power intake, skipping the PAK stage. A PPD is still conducted, by Qualifications |

This is the Qualified Lead scenario described in [[§4-4.3]]. No Mobility Evaluation needs scheduling, because one has already happened.

> **Watch-out — ask whether they've already seen their doctor about it.** A caller who mentions a recent appointment about mobility may be a QL rather than a standard lead. That's the difference between scheduling an evaluation and sending an order straight to Qualifications. A stale evaluation doesn't qualify, though: if the face-to-face is more than 6 months old, a new appointment is needed and the order follows the standard path — see [[§4-3.3]].

## §9-3 Where an order sits — Eligibility Status

The `Eligibility` tab of the Transaction Workflow shows where a PMD order is. This is the quickest way to tell whether it's still with Sales or has moved to Power.

{{diagram:eligibility-status}}

| Status | What it means | Whose it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Pending (Appoint. Req.)** | An appointment is required. Nothing arranged yet | Sales |
| **Pending (Appt. Sched.)** | Scheduling is underway — either by the patient, or by the Sales team on their behalf | Sales |
| **Pending (Appoint. Verification)** | A date and time **are** scheduled, but still need confirming by one or both parties — the patient and the provider's office | Sales |
| **Pending (Payer Verification)** | The Sales Eligibility team is confirming whether we can proceed on the patient's insurance | Sales Eligibility |
| **Eligible** | Sales is done. **The order is now with Power** | Power |

> **Watch-out — "Eligible" is the handoff marker.** If the status reads Eligible, don't route the caller to Sales. The order has left them. Use [[§4-1.1]] to work out where it is in the Power process instead.

## §9-4 A caller interested in a PMD

Check the **name and date of birth** first, as usual. Then **search their phone number in UMS** for an existing lead, while **asking whether they've already spoken with a team member**.

| What you find | What to do |
|---|---|
| **A lead exists** and is not closed | Check whether the most recent Sales representative is available for a direct transfer. If not, call the **Sales Q** |
| **No lead or record exists** — a brand-new lead | Call the **Sales Q**, tell them a new patient is calling, give them the **phone number and full name**, and transfer — see [[§9-5.1]] |

> **Note — ask as well as search.** The phone number search and the question are both needed. A patient may have spoken to someone using a different number, or a family member's.

## §9-5 Leads with no order yet

> **Policy** — When transferring a call from a **lead with no order yet created**, post the standard notation on the **Lead Resolutions** page.

A lead with no transaction hasn't reached step 4 of [[§9-2]], so there is nothing to check on the Eligibility tab. The Lead Resolutions page is the record.

Use the same format and wording as any call note — see [[§0-11.1]].

A quick tutorial for updating Lead Resolutions is in [[§9-8]].

### §9-5.1 A brand-new lead

When the caller has no record of any kind:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Search the **name and date of birth** |
| 2 | Search the **caller's phone number** |
| 3 | If no record appears, **transfer to the Sales Q**, giving them the caller's phone number and full name |

## §9-6 Re-opening a stale PMD order

> **Policy — the one-year PPD rule.** Check the PPD date on the order: `Pres. Forms` → **PPD Completed**.
>
> | PPD date | What happens |
> |---|---|
> | **Over 1 year old** | A **new order** is created, via the **Sales team** |
> | **Under 1 year old** | The **Power team** can re-open and resume the existing order, including scheduling a new appointment if needed |

> **Watch-out — this determines which department the call goes to.** A patient who "already started this a while back" may be either case, and the two paths are completely different — one restarts from Sales, the other resumes in Power.

Background on the PPD stage is in [[§4-3.1]].

## §9-7 Other Sales details

### §9-7.1 Insurance Member ID callbacks

If a caller is returning a call to provide their insurance Member ID:

1. Add the information to the transaction
2. Leave a note with the information

> **Watch-out — check the notes first.** Confirm the Member ID is actually all that was needed. If anything else is outstanding, transfer to the appropriate Sales team member instead — they may have further questions for the caller, and a second callback is worse than a transfer.

General guidance on insurance changes is in [[§0-3.2]].

### §9-7.2 Fax numbers

Sales often uses a department fax number: **210-761-7887**.

It is still acceptable to default to the main UMS fax number for referrals — **972-572-1112**. See [[§0-11.4]].

> **Note — when the main fax doesn't seem to arrive.** Occasionally an MDO reports sending documents to the main fax number several times without our receiving them, even though our digital fax system appears to be working normally. If they've already tried more than once, the Sales fax number can be given as a last resort.
