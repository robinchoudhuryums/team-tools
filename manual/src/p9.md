# Chapter 9 — After Hours


> **About this draft.** After Hours is its own chapter rather than a section of Chapter 0. Chapter 0 appears in full in every extract, and this is a distinct operating mode used by whoever covers the shift — not something every CSR needs in front of them on every daytime call.
>
> The on-call contacts render from `roster.json`, so a change to the roster updates every artifact on the next build.

## §9-1 When this applies

After-hours calls may be received:

- **5:00 PM – 8:00 AM Monday to Friday** (Central Time)
- **Any time** on weekends and company holidays

> **Critical — emergencies come first.** The emergency instruction in [[§1-4.1]] is the first question on every after-hours call and overrides everything in this chapter.

### §9-1.1 Who can actually be dispatched

> **Policy — after-hours dispatch is DFW only.** Both respiratory therapists and the DME technicians operate out of the **Irving office**, so on-call dispatch is available to patients in the **DFW area**, with one exception in San Antonio.

This is the first thing to establish after the emergency question. Outside the areas below no one can be dispatched, and telling a caller someone is coming is worse than telling them the truth early.

| Patient location | Respiratory therapist | DME technician |
|---|---|---|
| DFW | Yes | Yes |
| San Antonio | No | **Oxygen concentrator service only** |
| Hawaii | No | No |
| Anywhere else | No | No |

### §9-1.2 The on-call rota

Respiratory therapists and DME technicians **alternate weeks** on call, within their own groups. The two RTs — `[ROLE: On-Call Respiratory Therapist]` — alternate with each other; the Irving technicians alternate among themselves. Contacts are in [[§9-5]]; the current week's assignment is in the After Hours Call Log.

> **Watch-out** — Always check the schedule before contacting a technician or RT to dispatch for after-hours assistance.

### §9-1.3 Before the shift starts

- ☐ **Cell phone and charger** — fully charged before the shift
- ☐ **Laptop and charger** — access to the UMS system
- ☐ **Logbook** — every call and service completion is logged

## §9-2 Call handling

> **Script — opening.** "Thank you for calling UniversalMed Supply after hours service, how may I help you?"

### §9-2.1 What to establish, in order

| Question | If the answer is… |
|---|---|
| **Is this a medical emergency?** | **Yes → advise 9-1-1 immediately.** Stop here |
| New or existing patient? | New → advise them to call during normal business hours |
| Caller name and relationship | If not the patient, note the relationship |
| Patient name, DOB, phone, **address** | Verify in the UMS system — identity verification still applies. A family member or caregiver confirms their relationship and the patient's details, per [[§1-3]]. **The address determines whether anyone can be dispatched** — see [[§9-1.1]] |
| Equipment involved | All equipment related to the issue |
| Any insurance or payment conflicts? | Confirm nothing prevents service |
| **Was the equipment provided by UMS?** | Confirm it hasn't been picked up. **If it isn't our equipment, we cannot assist** |

## §9-3 Dispatch by equipment type

| Equipment | Contact | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxygen concentrator, suction machine** | On-call DME technician | **Troubleshoot first** using the O2 troubleshooting steps ([[§8-6]]) or the suction setup guide. Dispatch only if unresolved |
| **Ventilator, cough assist, IPPB, BiPAP ST** | On-call respiratory therapist | **Dispatch immediately. Do not troubleshoot complex respiratory equipment** |
| **Power wheelchair** | — | Advise calling back during business hours. If the patient is stuck in a vulnerable situation, advise them to contact local non-emergency services or 9-1-1 |
| **Anything else** — order status, routine supplies, product inquiry | — | Advise calling during regular business hours. Log the call |

> **Watch-out — troubleshoot first.** A technician who arrives and resolves the issue through the standard troubleshooting steps that could have been done over the phone has been called out to the patient's home unnecessarily.

> **Watch-out — check the location before the equipment type.** The table above assumes a DFW patient. Outside DFW, the only after-hours dispatch is oxygen concentrator service in San Antonio. See [[§9-1.1]].

> **Watch-out — oxygen combined with another device.** If oxygen equipment is being used with a PAP or ventilator, that may change whether a DME technician or a respiratory therapist is better suited. Confirm before dispatching.

### §9-3.1 What to relay when dispatching

All of the following, every time — verbally if possible, **and** by text message:

| Detail | |
|---|---|
| Patient name | Required |
| Address | Required |
| Phone number | Required |
| Caller name, relationship and phone, if different | Required where applicable |
| Type of equipment | Required |
| Type of issue | Required |
| **Troubleshooting already attempted** | Required — describe the steps taken |

## §9-4 Escalation chain

If the on-call technician or RT doesn't answer:

{{diagram:afterhours-escalation}}

| Step | Action | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call the on-call DME technician or RT **and** send them a text message with the collected on-call details ([[§9-3.1]]) | Wait 30 minutes |
| 2 | No response → call again and re-send the text message | Wait 30 minutes |
| 3 | Still nothing → call **and** text Level 1 Backup. **For an RT call** — ventilator, cough assist, IPPB, BiPAP ST — call the **other RT** instead; Level 1 is a DME technician. See [[§9-5]] | Wait 30 minutes |
| 4 | No response → call Level 2 Backup, if one is listed | Wait 30 minutes |
| 5 | No one responds → **repeat from step 1**, and keep cycling until someone responds. If distress develops at any point, 9-1-1 comes first | Ongoing |

> **Policy — do not contact off-schedule staff.** Only the scheduled on-call personnel and the designated backup contacts.

> **Note — not acknowledged until they confirm.** Don't consider the situation acknowledged or being addressed until you receive a verbal or written confirmation from the technician or RT. They're expected to send a message to the on-call phone once the issue is resolved, to close the loop — see [[§9-9]].

### §9-4.1 Response time targets

| Priority | Target | Equipment |
|---|---|---|
| **Immediate** | 0–60 minutes | Ventilators, oxygen concentrators, suction machines |
| Quick | 1–2 hours | Other equipment needing a prompt response |

> **Note** — The target timeframe is what we aim for, but is not guaranteed.

### §9-4.2 While the chain is being worked

The chain can run for a long time. What happens with the **caller** during that window matters as much as the dispatch attempts.

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Confirm the patient has a working backup — a spare cylinder, or a second device | This is what makes the wait survivable |
| Tell the caller plainly that you are reaching the on-call staff and will call back | Silence reads as nothing happening |
| Check back in with the patient contact periodically, even if there is no update yet | |
| **If the patient develops any immediate breathing difficulty or distress, stop and tell them to dial 9-1-1** | See [[§9-1]] |

> **Critical — a long escalation is not a reason to wait.** If at any point during the chain the patient is in immediate distress, 9-1-1 comes first. The dispatch attempt continues afterward; it does not come first.

## §9-6 Voicemail

If a call is missed and a voicemail is left, call back **within the response time targets in [[§9-4.1]]**.

> **Script — returning a voicemail.** "Hello, this is [name] with UniversalMed Supply returning your call regarding one of our patients. Can you confirm the date of birth and name, and tell me more about the issue?"

## §9-7 After the service is complete

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Receive confirmation from the technician or RT that service is complete |
| 2 | **Create a service ticket in UMS** with full detail of the issue and the resolution. On the `Ticket` tab, set `Service Type` to **After Hours** |
| 3 | **Log the service visit** in the After-Hours Logbook |

> **Watch-out — both records are required.** The ticket is what the daytime team sees; the logbook is the after-hours record. Doing one and not the other leaves a gap on Monday morning.

## §9-9 For on-call technicians and RTs

This section is for the respiratory therapist or DME technician who takes the dispatch, not the person working the call.

> **Note — phone first.** For respiratory therapists and DME technicians: first attempt to contact the patient and troubleshoot over the phone, in case it wasn't already attempted or it resolves the issue without an in-person visit. This applies to RTs on ventilator calls too — the person working the call still dispatches a ventilator call immediately and never troubleshoots it, and the RT's own first step is to call the patient.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| You receive the dispatch | Confirm it to the on-call phone, by call or text. Until you do, it is treated as unacknowledged and the escalation chain keeps running — [[§9-4]] |
| The issue is resolved | Send a message to the on-call phone to close the loop, so the service ticket and logbook can be completed — [[§9-7]] |
