# Part 8 — After Hours


> **About this draft.** After Hours is its own part rather than a section of Part 0. Part 0 appears in full in every extract, and this is a distinct operating mode used by whoever covers the shift — not something every CSR needs in front of them on every daytime call.
>
> The on-call contacts render from `roster.json`, so a change to the roster updates every artifact on the next build.

## §8-1 When this applies

After-hours calls may be received:

- **5:00 PM – 8:00 AM Monday to Friday** (Central Time)
- **Any time** on weekends and company holidays

> **Critical — emergencies come first.** The emergency instruction in [[§1-4.1]] is the first question on every after-hours call and overrides everything in this part.

### §8-1.1 Who can actually be dispatched

> **Policy — after-hours dispatch is DFW only.** Both respiratory therapists and the DME technicians operate out of the **Irving office**, so on-call dispatch is available to patients in the **DFW area**.
>
> **One exception:** a DME technician is available in **San Antonio for oxygen concentrator service**. Nothing else.

This is the first thing to establish after the emergency question. A patient outside DFW with a ventilator issue cannot have someone dispatched, and telling them one is coming is worse than telling them the truth early.

| Patient location | After-hours dispatch |
|---|---|
| DFW | Yes — RT or DME technician by equipment type |
| San Antonio | **Oxygen concentrator service only** |
| Hawaii | **None.** There is no after-hours dispatch in Hawaii |
| Anywhere else | None |

### §8-1.2 The on-call rota

Respiratory therapists and DME technicians **alternate weeks** on call, within their own groups. The two RTs — **Ursula Collins** and **Menzise Richardson** — alternate with each other; the Irving technicians alternate among themselves. The current week's assignment is in the After Hours Call Log.

> **Watch-out** — Check the schedule at the start of every shift. Who was on call last week is not who is on call this week.

### §8-1.3 Before the shift starts

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Cell phone and charger | Fully charged before the shift |
| Laptop and charger | Access to the UMS system |
| Logbook | Every call and service completion is logged |
| **On-call schedule** | In the After Hours Call Log. **Check it at the start of each shift** to confirm who is on call |

## §8-2 Call handling

> **Script — opening.** "Thank you for calling UniversalMed Supply after hours service, how may I help you?"

### §8-2.1 What to establish, in order

| Question | If the answer is… |
|---|---|
| **Is this a medical emergency?** | **Yes → advise 9-1-1 immediately.** Stop here |
| New or existing patient? | New → advise them to call during normal business hours |
| Caller name and relationship | If not the patient, note the relationship |
| Patient name, DOB, phone, **address** | Verify in the UMS system — identity verification still applies. A family member or caregiver confirms their relationship and the patient's details, per [[§1-3]]. **The address determines whether anyone can be dispatched** — see [[§8-1.1]] |
| Equipment involved | All equipment related to the issue |
| Any insurance or payment conflicts? | Confirm nothing prevents service |
| **Was the equipment provided by UMS?** | Confirm it hasn't been picked up. **If it isn't our equipment, we cannot assist** |

## §8-3 Dispatch by equipment type

| Equipment | Contact | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxygen concentrator, suction machine** | On-call DME technician | **Troubleshoot first** using the O2 troubleshooting steps ([[§7-6]]) or the suction setup guide. Dispatch only if unresolved |
| **Ventilator, cough assist, IPPB, BiPAP ST** | On-call respiratory therapist | **Dispatch immediately. Do not troubleshoot complex respiratory equipment** |
| **Power wheelchair** | — | Advise calling back during business hours. If the patient is stuck in a vulnerable situation, advise non-emergency services or 9-1-1 |
| **Anything else** — order status, routine supplies, product inquiry | — | Advise calling during regular business hours. Log the call |

> **Watch-out — check the location before the equipment type.** The table above assumes a DFW patient. Outside DFW, the only after-hours dispatch is oxygen concentrator service in San Antonio. See [[§8-1.1]].

> **Watch-out — oxygen combined with another device.** If oxygen equipment is being used with a PAP or ventilator, that may change whether a DME technician or a respiratory therapist is better suited. Confirm before dispatching.

### §8-3.1 What to relay when dispatching

All of the following, every time:

| Detail | |
|---|---|
| Patient name | Required |
| Address | Required |
| Phone number | Required |
| Caller name, relationship and phone, if different | Required where applicable |
| Type of equipment | Required |
| Type of issue | Required |
| **Troubleshooting already attempted** | Required — describe the steps taken |

> **Watch-out** — The troubleshooting summary is the field most often skipped and the one that saves the most time. A technician who arrives and repeats what was already tried has spent a call-out for nothing.

## §8-4 Escalation chain

If the on-call technician or RT doesn't answer:

{{diagram:afterhours-escalation}}

| Step | Action | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call the on-call DME technician or RT | Immediately |
| 2 | No answer → send a text message | Wait 30 minutes |
| 3 | Still nothing → call **and** text Level 1 Backup. **For an RT call** — ventilator, cough assist, IPPB, BiPAP ST — call the **other RT** instead; Level 1 is a DME technician. See [[§8-5]] | Wait 30 minutes |
| 4 | No response → call Level 2 Backup, if one is listed | Wait 30 minutes |
| 5 | No one responds → **repeat from step 1**. If distress develops at any point, 9-1-1 comes first | Ongoing |

> **Policy — do not contact off-schedule staff.** Only the scheduled on-call personnel and the designated backup contacts. Not technicians or therapists who happen to be reachable.

> **Note — the target is what we aim for, not what the chain guarantees.** The 0–60 minute target assumes someone answers. Working steps 1 through 4 takes at least 90 minutes on its own, so a call where nobody responds will exceed the target no matter how it's handled.
>
> When that happens there is no shortcut: **keep cycling the chain until someone responds.** The same applies to the on-call technician. Do not contact off-schedule staff to fill the gap.

### §8-4.1 Response time targets

| Priority | Target | Equipment |
|---|---|---|
| **Immediate** | 0–60 minutes | Ventilators, oxygen concentrators, suction machines |
| Quick | 1–2 hours | Other equipment needing a prompt response |

### §8-4.2 While the chain is being worked

The chain can run for a long time. What happens with the **caller** during that window matters as much as the dispatch attempts.

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Confirm the patient has a working backup — a spare cylinder, or a second device | This is what makes the wait survivable |
| Tell the caller plainly that you are reaching the on-call staff and will call back | Silence reads as nothing happening |
| Call back with an update even when there is no news | |
| **If the patient develops any immediate breathing difficulty or distress, stop and tell them to dial 9-1-1** | See [[§8-1]] |

> **Critical — a long escalation is not a reason to wait.** If at any point during the chain the patient is in immediate distress, 9-1-1 comes first. The dispatch attempt continues afterward; it does not come first.

## §8-6 Voicemail

If a call is missed and a voicemail is left, call back **within the response time targets in [[§8-4.1]]**.

> **Script — returning a voicemail.** "Hello, this is [name] with UniversalMed Supply returning your call regarding one of our patients. Can you confirm the date of birth and name, and tell me more about the issue?"

## §8-7 After the service is complete

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Receive confirmation from the technician or RT that service is complete |
| 2 | **Create a service ticket in UMS** with full detail of the issue and the resolution |
| 3 | **Log the service visit** in the After-Hours Logbook |

> **Watch-out — both records are required.** The ticket is what the daytime team sees; the logbook is the after-hours record. Doing one and not the other leaves a gap on Monday morning.
