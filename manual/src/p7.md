## §7-1 Scope and call types

> **Policy — the governing rule.** Oxygen supplies are **only** delivered to patients who received an oxygen concentrator from us. We do not supply tanks or accessories to patients using another company's concentrator.

| Call type | CSR responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen supply order** | Most common. CSRs create these as service tickets — see [[§7-2]] |
| **Concentrator service or fault** | Handled by CSR, **not** the Service team. See [[§7-6]] |
| **New patient inquiry, no order in the system** | Advise their provider to send a referral. Resupply processes the initial order — documents and insurance — and CSR work starts once the concentrator is delivered. See [[§3-3]] |
| **Portable oxygen concentrator request** | We do not carry POCs. Only home units. Offer a home unit if they're interested |
| **Patient has a concentrator from another supplier** | They must return it to that supplier, then have their provider send us a referral for a concentrator **and** supplies. We will not supply tanks alone |

## §7-2 Oxygen supply tickets

CSRs create oxygen supply orders as service tickets. The steps, with screenshots, are in [[§7-2.2]].

### §7-2.1 Ordering rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Delivery lead time** | **2 business days** from the order date. Exception: same-day or next-day drop-off or pick-up at the Irving office during business hours |
| **Order frequency** | Tanks may be ordered **once per week**. Scheduling ahead is fine if the delivery date is at least a week from the previous delivery |
| **Maximum per delivery** | Hard cap of **10** in any combination of M6 and E. The general limit is **6** — only approve more if the patient has routinely received that many or the provider states the need. Confirm with a supervisor if the patient insists on more than 6 |
| **Empty tank return** | The same number of empties should be picked up as full tanks delivered, to avoid inventory problems. One extra delivered is usually acceptable within the cap of 10 |
| **Minimum at home** | Per insurance guidelines the patient should always have **at least one E cylinder** at home in case of a power outage |
| **Sizes carried** | M6 and E cylinders only |

> **Watch-out — this is not resupply.** Oxygen tanks are a rental accessory, not a resupply item. The 30-day contact and 10-day shipping windows in [[§5-8.1]] do **not** apply. The governing limit here is once per week with a 2-business-day lead time.

### §7-2.2 Ticket creation

| Step | Action |

## §7-3 Urgent and after-hours requests

### §7-3.1 Same-day tank delivery

> **Policy** — If a patient says they urgently need tanks, the oxygen ticket **can be created as same-day**. Advise them that our standard policy is a 2-business-day lead time and that they should call earlier going forward.

> **Script.** "I can get this out to you today. Going forward, we do ask for two business days' notice on tank orders so we can make sure it's scheduled — it helps us avoid situations where you're running low."

### §7-3.2 Power outage

A patient without power and needing oxygen urgently can have an oxygen ticket created for **same-day service**. This is the situation the at-home E cylinder requirement exists for — check whether they have it before anything else.

### §7-3.3 After hours

There is an **after-hours line on the same phone number**. The employee covering it can create a ticket and contact the **on-call respiratory therapist** or **on-call technician**, depending on the equipment, and dispatch them to the patient immediately.

### §7-3.4 Genuine emergencies

> **Critical** — the emergency instruction in [[§1-4.1]] applies to every oxygen call. If the patient
> is in immediate distress, **9-1-1 comes before anything in this part**, including troubleshooting.

On oxygen calls specifically, the first thing to check once distress is ruled out is whether the
patient has a working backup — a spare cylinder.

## §7-4 Monthly supply allowances

{{eq:oxygen-supply|capacity_lbs,seat,dimensions,product_weight_lbs,billing_type}}

> **Note — cannulas include tubing.** Nasal cannulas come in packs of 4 and include 7 ft tubing, so patients do not need to request 7 ft tubing separately. Longer runs — 25 ft and 50 ft — are requested separately, and tubing can be cut to a shorter custom length on request.

> **Watch-out — humidifier bottles.** Always add humidifier adapter tubing to the order when ordering a bottle.

### §7-4.1 Accessories are included in the rental

> **Policy — no separate charge, for the full 5 years.** Per CMS Policy Article A52514, oxygen accessories are **included in the monthly rental allowance** during the 36-month rental period. After month 36, the supplier must **keep providing** them — along with the equipment, maintenance and repair — for the rest of the 5-year reasonable useful lifetime, with no separate payment. The supplier provides any accessory ordered by the treating practitioner at no additional charge throughout.
>
> Covered by this: cannulas (A4615), tubing (A4616), **standard regulators (E1353)**, **oxygen conserving devices (A9900)** when ordered, humidifiers (E0555), and stands and racks (E1355).
>
> A patient should **not** see separate bills for these supplies at any point in the 5 years — including after the rental billing stops.

### §7-5.3 Conserving device

{{figure-pair:o2-regulator|o2-conserver}}

> **Policy — the conserver is included, not billed.** For a Medicare patient, an oxygen conserving device is an accessory **included in the oxygen rental**. If the patient's provider orders one, we provide it **at no charge**. It is never billed to insurance separately.
>
> Without an order, the patient can buy one out of pocket for **$320** — Medicare patients sign an ABN first. Otherwise they receive the standard regulator.

> **Watch-out — the conserver is not the standard regulator.** Every patient gets the **standard regulator (E1353)**. The **conserver (A9900)** is an advanced regulator, supplied only with a provider's order or bought out of pocket. Don't conflate them when a patient asks about "the regulator."

Releases oxygen in pulses on inhalation rather than constant flow, substantially extending tank life — compare the pulse dose and continuous flow rows in [[§7-5.2]].

> **Policy — when to raise it.** If a patient has 6 or more tanks but is running out unusually fast, first investigate whether a regulator or tubing fault is causing quick depletion. Then inform them about the conserving device.

## §7-6 Concentrator troubleshooting

> **Policy** — When a patient reports a concentrator issue, **the CSR troubleshoots it.** This does not go to the Service team. See [[§6-5.2]].

{{diagram:o2-troubleshoot}}

**First, confirm it's our equipment.** Verify we provided the concentrator and that it is actively billing. If it isn't ours, the patient should call their actual provider.

| Symptom | Action |
|---|---|
| **Does not power up** | Ensure it's plugged **directly into a wall outlet** — not an extension cord, not a wall-switch outlet, not a multi-plug adapter. Test a different outlet. Still no power → schedule a tech visit |
| **Yellow light at startup** | Normal for the first 15 minutes. Beyond that → schedule service on a regular weekday |
| **Alarm or red light after power-up** | Have the patient remove **all** tubing from the machine. If flow returns to normal with no overheating, it's a tubing issue — replace the tubing. Still faulty → schedule a tech visit |
| **Water in the tubing** | Usually an overfilled humidifier bottle. Have them check the max line and keep water below it. Remove the bottle and test whether the machine runs without it |
| **General alarm** | Check the power cord is secure, check for kinks in the tubing, confirm it isn't on a wall-switch outlet. Turn off, wait 60 seconds, turn back on. Still alarming → the patient should **switch to a cylinder** and contact us |

> **Critical — if the patient is in distress.** Troubleshooting assumes a patient who is stable and on a backup supply. If at any point they describe immediate breathing difficulty, stop and follow [[§7-3.4]].

### §7-6.1 Maintenance

Patients should clean the concentrator filter at least **weekly** — wash with tap water, squeeze dry with a clean towel. UMS schedules a maintenance visit every **6 months**.

## §7-7 Safety guidelines

Oxygen is a prescription item. Patients must follow their prescribed liter flow and duration.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Post "No Smoking / Oxygen in Use" signs | Allow open flames or smoking in the same room |
| Keep a fire extinguisher and smoke detector in the home | Use electrical equipment that may spark within 5 ft |
| Have an evacuation plan ready | Use flammable products nearby — cleaning fluid, paint thinner, aerosols, oil-based creams, Vaseline |
| Keep cylinders in a stand or cart, belted, or lying securely | Store oxygen in confined spaces — closets, car trunks |
| Store portable oxygen securely in the **back** of a car | Place the concentrator against a wall or curtains, blocking air circulation |
| Keep cylinders away from heat sources | Cut or splice tubing, or use more than 50 ft |
| Change the cannula every 2–4 weeks, or after illness | Change the flow rate without physician or UMS direction |
| Change tubing every 3–6 months, or immediately if damaged | Wait until the last minute to reorder — order at least **2 business days** ahead |

### §7-7.1 Using a cylinder

{{figure:o2-cyl-2}}

{{figure:o2-cyl-1}}

| Step | Instruction |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ensure the flow regulator knob is at zero |
| 2 | Make sure the T-handle is tight |
| 3 | Place the cylinder wrench on the on/off valve at the top |
| 4 | Open the valve counter-clockwise one full turn. The gauge should read ~2,000 PSI when full |
| 5 | Adjust the flow regulator to the prescribed rate |
| 6 | Attach tubing to the nipple adaptor on the regulator |

**Changing a cylinder.** Check the pressure gauge frequently. When the needle reaches the lower part of the red section — below about 200 PSI — it's time to change. Turn the on/off valve clockwise to close, bleed off pressure by opening the flow regulator, and when the gauge reads zero, return the regulator knob to zero.

## §7-8 Rental, compliance and prescriptions

### §7-8.1 The 36-month rule

> **Policy — the patient does not own the concentrator.** Medicare pays a monthly rental for **36 months**. After that, rental billing stops, but **UMS keeps ownership** and must continue furnishing the equipment, supplies and servicing for the remainder of the 5-year reasonable useful lifetime.
>
> **Do not tell an oxygen patient they will own the concentrator after 36 months.** Say: "Medicare pays for the rental for 36 months. After that, you keep using the equipment at no rental charge and we continue to service it, for up to 5 years total."

Full billing detail is in [[§10-13.2]].

### §7-8.2 Compliance

Concentrators are subject to ongoing compliance. Updated medical documentation must be provided periodically by the patient's provider for insurance to keep paying.

> **Policy** — If a patient is out of compliance we cannot provide related supplies or services until it is resolved. Persistent non-compliance after multiple attempts may result in equipment pick-up.

Check the compliance specialists' notes on the account first — they usually say whether the patient is compliant, whether documents are being requested, or whether non-compliance has been confirmed. Transfer to `[ROLE: Compliance Specialist]` only if the notes don't settle it. See [[§3-12]].

### §7-8.3 Prescription requirement

A prescription is required **even for out-of-pocket purchase** — this overrides the general rule in [[§0-6]]. The prescription specifies liter flow and duration of use. Patients must never change their flow rate without physician direction.

### §7-8.4 Facility stays

A hospital or SNF stay is **not** a break in need — see [[§7-9]]. But during a Medicare **Part A covered SNF stay**, the facility is responsible for the patient's oxygen and UMS cannot bill for it. See [[§5-4.2]].

We also cannot deliver oxygen supplies to a hospital. See [[§5-4.3]].

## §7-9 Restart rules

When a new 36-month rental period can begin. This is the most misunderstood area of oxygen billing.

### §7-9.1 Break in need — short

If oxygen use ends for **less than 60 days plus the remainder of the rental month of discontinuation** and then resumes, payment resumes where it left off. No new rental period, no new qualifying test, no new documentation.

> **Policy — the calculation includes the remainder of the month.** The threshold is 60 days **plus** the days remaining in the rental month when use ceased.
>
> Example: oxygen discontinued 10 March leaves 21 days remaining in March. The break must exceed **81 days** for a new period to start. Resuming on or before 30 May picks up where it left off; 1 June or later starts a new period.

### §7-9.2 Break in need — long, during months 1–36

If use ends for **more than** that threshold and new medical necessity is established, a **new 36-month period begins**, requiring:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| New prescription | Detailed written order; WOPD if required |
| New qualifying blood gas study | ABG or pulse oximetry within 30 days prior to the new initial date |
| Face-to-face evaluation | Within 30 days of the new initial certification |
| Documentation of new medical need | Clinical notes supporting why oxygen is needed again |
| Documentation of the interruption | Cause, length, pick-up date of the old equipment, new delivery date |
| Claim narrative | First month's claim must state "Break in medical need greater than 60 days" |

### §7-9.3 Break in need — during months 37–60

A new rental period does **not** begin. The supplier who provided oxygen during the 36th rental month must continue providing all necessary items and services for the remainder of the 5-year RUL.

### §7-9.4 What restarts the 36 months

| Situation | New 36-month period? | New qualifying test? |
|---|---|---|
| Break ≤ 60 days plus remainder of month | No — resumes | No |
| Break > 60 days during months 1–36 | **Yes** | **Yes** — within 30 days of the new start |
| Break > 60 days during months 37–60 | No — original supplier continues | No |
| Equipment reaches 5-year RUL and the patient elects new equipment | **Yes** | No — most recent qualifying test accepted |
| Equipment lost, stolen, or irreparably damaged — see [[§6-10.1]] for what counts | **Yes** | No — most recent test accepted |
| Supplier abandons the patient, or Chapter 7 bankruptcy | **Yes** | No — most recent test accepted |
| Hospital or SNF stay, patient still needs oxygen | No — resumes | No |
| Switch from Medicare Advantage to Fee-for-Service | No — resumes, with documentation | No — most recent test accepted |
| Change in equipment modality or supplier | No | No |
| Malfunction, wear and tear, or routine repair | No | No |

> **Note — MA to FFS.** A new blood gas study is not required. The most recent qualifying study the patient previously obtained is used, per DME MAC policy.

### §7-9.5 The 5-year RUL

The reasonable useful lifetime is **5 years from the initial date of service** — not the age of the equipment. After it expires the patient may elect new equipment, starting a new 36-month period. The 5 years does **not** restart for modality changes, equipment swap-outs, or supplier changes.

> **Policy — replacement is all or nothing.** If the patient elects replacement after the RUL, **both stationary and portable equipment must be replaced at the same time**. A new 36-month period and a new RUL begin for both. The first month's claim needs the RA modifier, but no new qualifying blood gas testing.

## §7-10 FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the nose piece come with tubing? | Yes. Cannulas come in packs of 4 including 7 ft tubing. Longer runs are requested separately |
| Can I buy a concentrator out of pocket? | Yes, but a prescription is still required. See [[§7-8.3]] |
| Do you have portable oxygen concentrators? | No. Home units only, 5 LPM and 10 LPM |
| How often can I order tanks? | Once per week, with 2 business days' notice — or same/next day for Irving office pickup. See [[§7-2.1]] |
| I need tanks today. | We can create the ticket as same-day. Going forward, please allow two business days. See [[§7-3.1]] |
| My power is out and I need oxygen. | Check whether they have their E cylinder, then create a same-day ticket. See [[§7-3.2]] |
| My oxygen runs out too quickly. | Check for regulator or tubing faults first, then consider E tanks or the conserving device. See [[§7-5.3]] |
| Can I get tanks if my concentrator came from another company? | No. We only supply oxygen to patients who received a concentrator from us. See [[§7-1]] |
| How do I clean my concentrator? | Weekly filter clean — tap water, squeeze dry. We schedule a maintenance visit every 6 months |
| My concentrator stopped working. | Switch to a cylinder and contact us. We troubleshoot by phone and schedule a tech visit if needed. See [[§7-6]] |
| Will I own my concentrator after 36 months? | **No.** Rental billing stops at 36 months but we keep ownership and keep servicing it for up to 5 years total. See [[§7-8.1]] |
| I stopped oxygen and need it again — does it restart? | Depends on the break length. See [[§7-9]] |
| My equipment is 5+ years old — can I get new? | Yes, after the RUL. No new blood gas test needed, but both stationary and portable are replaced together. See [[§7-9.5]] |
| I was hospitalized — does the rental restart? | No. A hospital or SNF stay where the patient still needs oxygen is not a break in need. We also can't deliver oxygen supplies to a hospital |
