## §3-2 Department specific amendments

Everything in Chapter 0 applies here, with one exception specific to respiratory equipment — plus a note on pick-ups, which follow the normal process.

> **Policy — prescription required for out-of-pocket purchases.** [[§0-6]] says out-of-pocket purchases need no paperwork because we aren't billing insurance. **Oxygen concentrators, PAP devices (CPAP and BiPAP), and ventilators require a prescription regardless** — federal law restricts their sale to a prescriber's order.

> **Note — picking up a respiratory machine.** Create the pick-up ticket as for any other equipment, unless the item is patient-owned — you don't need a discontinue prescription to create it. Field Ops reviews every pick-up ticket and decides whether anything more is needed, such as documents from the MDO.

## §3-3 Routine resupply

The Resupply team handles orders for routine shipment of disposable items — briefs, trach tubes, PAP supplies — and **processes the initial order** for respiratory machines, including oxygen concentrators and ventilators: requesting and reviewing the provider's documents, and submitting to insurance.

Once a machine is delivered, the work moves elsewhere:

| After delivery | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| Oxygen concentrator service, oxygen supplies | CSR — service tickets. See [[§8-1]] |
| A problem with a ventilator | Service Q — see [[§3-10]] |

Designated team members own each category:

- Tracheostomy, suction and ventilator supplies — `[ROLE: Resupply Category Owner — Tracheostomy / Suction / Ventilator supplies]`
- Incontinence and catheter supplies — `[ROLE: Resupply Category Owner — Incontinence / Catheter supplies]`
- PAP supplies — `[ROLE: Resupply Category Owner — CPAP supplies]`

> **Note** — The current owners are in the directory, [[§B-1]]. Also check the notes, to see who has been handling the patient's order.

### §3-3.1 When the category owner is unavailable

1. Confirm the patient's current address, insurance, prescribing provider, and the items needed
2. Ask whether they want the same items as the previous order (often the case)
3. If yes, email the Resupply team using the **Repeat Resupply** update type in the email tool. If they want different items, email the team with the standard subject format from [[§0-11.2]] and list what's needed

The Repeat Resupply form asks for:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Item Category | The supply category — CPAP, Trach, Incontinence, for example |
| D.O.B. | The patient's date of birth (optional) |
| Requesting Month | The month the supplies are for |
| Last Scheduled Date | When the previous shipment was scheduled |
| Same items & quantity as previous | Yes, or what's different |
| Address | Same, or Different with the new address |
| Insurance | Same, or Changed with the new plan and member ID |
| Provider | Same, or Changed with the new provider and phone |
| Note | Anything else the team needs to know |

Subject format: `[Category] [Month] Resupply: [Patient name], DOB: [date]` — for example, `CPAP March Resupply: Jane Doe, DOB: 01/02/1950`.

### §3-3.2 A note on allowances

The allowances in the tables below are the standard quantities insurance will cover per period. Two things worth knowing when a patient asks for more:

- **Allowances other than incontinence follow Medicare's published limits.** Any items exceeding the allowance may require paying an out-of-pocket cost — for a Medicare patient, only after a signed ABN
- **Incontinence supplies are the exception.** Medicare does not cover incontinence supplies at all — these are Texas Medicaid only, and their allowances follow TMHP rather than Medicare

> **Policy — no more than three months at a time.** Whatever the allowance, a supplier may not dispense more than a three-month quantity of supplies in one shipment.

> **Note** — Texas Medicaid and MCO resupply shipping-window rules may differ — see [[§6-8.1]].

## §3-7 PAP (Positive Airway Pressure)

PAP devices treat sleep apnea and other breathing problems by pumping air into the airway to prevent obstruction.

| Device | How it works | Typically for |
|---|---|---|
| **CPAP** | Constant pressure | Standard obstructive sleep apnea |
| **BiPAP** | Higher pressure on inhalation, lower on exhalation | More severe conditions |

> **Policy** — PAP devices are subject to compliance monitoring. See [[§3-7.4]].

### §3-7.1 CPAP machines (E0601)

{{eq:cpap|hcpcs,capacity_lbs,seat,allowance}}

**APAP.** An auto-adjusting CPAP (APAP) bills under the same code as a CPAP — **E0601** — so documentation for E0601 covers either. An order that comes in for an APAP, or a patient or provider's office asking for an APAP instead of a CPAP, doesn't need a different code or new documents for the device. The **Luna G3 X APAP**, in the table above, works as either a CPAP or an APAP. The pressure setting itself still comes from the provider.

### §3-7.2 BiPAP machines

{{eq:bipap|capacity_lbs,seat,allowance,notes}}

*E0470 vs. E0471 — the difference is the back-up rate.*

| Code | Back-up rate | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| E0470 | No | Delivers bi-level pressure in response to the patient's own breathing |
| E0471 | Yes | Delivers a pre-set breathing rate if the patient's spontaneous breaths fall below a threshold |

The prescribing provider determines which applies.[^billing] A patient asking to "upgrade" to E0471 needs a new prescription, not an order change.[^switch]

[^billing]: Billing differs too. E0601 and E0470 are capped rentals: 13 paid months, then the patient owns the device. **E0471 is a continuous monthly rental** in Medicare's frequent-and-substantial-servicing category — billed monthly for as long as it is medically necessary, and ownership never transfers. See [[§10-12]].
[^switch]: Switching devices: if an E0601 is tried and found ineffective during the initial titration or home trial, substituting an E0470 needs no new evaluation or sleep test. If the E0601 has been used for more than 3 months before the switch, a new in-person evaluation is required but a new sleep test is not.

### §3-7.4 PAP compliance

> **Policy — Medicare compliance standard. Two conditions, both required.**
>
> 1. **Usage.** The patient uses the device at least **4 hours per night on 70% of nights during any consecutive 30-day period** within the first 90 days — 21 of 30 nights. The device reports this to us automatically, via an app.[^app]
> 2. **Follow-up visit.** A face-to-face re-evaluation by the treating practitioner **between day 31 and day 91**, documenting that the patient is benefiting.
>
> The usage window is any consecutive 30 days inside the first 90 — not 70% across the whole 90 days. The visit window is strict at both ends: a visit on day 25 does not count.

[^app]: The people with access to the app include the patient's doctor, our respiratory therapists and our compliance specialists.

> **Watch-out — the follow-up visit is where coverage is most often lost.** A patient can meet every usage target and still lose coverage because the visit didn't happen inside the window. Tell PAP patients at delivery that both conditions apply, and encourage the appointment early in the range rather than near day 91.

> **Watch-out — if the trial fails.** For Medicare and Medicare Advantage, if the adherence standard isn't met within the first 3 months, Medicare stops paying. Requalifying needs both a new in-person evaluation and a repeat sleep study in a sleep lab — a home sleep test doesn't count. Medicaid and commercial plans set their own requirements, and some accept a home test; check the plan.

> **Script — patient asking about PAP compliance.** "Your insurance covers the CPAP on a trial basis for the first 90 days. Two things have to happen. The machine needs to show you're using it at least four hours a night on most nights over a thirty-day stretch — it sends us that automatically. And your doctor needs to see you for a follow-up visit during that period. If you're having trouble with the mask or the pressure, call us right away so we can help before the window closes."

## §3-12 Compliance

Four categories are subject to ongoing compliance, and three of them are respiratory: **oxygen
concentrators**, **PAP devices** and **ventilators**. The fourth, the low air loss mattress, is in [[§2-3.4]].

> **Policy — read the compliance notes first.** The compliance specialists leave notes on the account that usually answer the question: the date a patient is compliant through, that they're actively requesting documents from the provider, or that the patient is confirmed non-compliant and a pick-up has been initiated. Answer from those notes.
>
> If the notes don't confirm it, or the caller asks something you can't answer, transfer to `[ROLE: Compliance Specialist]`. Don't reach your own conclusion about compliance where the notes are silent.

### §3-12.1 Two kinds of compliance

Compliance is the ongoing verification of medical necessity for equipment that insurance covers on a continuing basis. **"Compliance" means two different things**, and the patient script has to match the item.

| Type | Items | Who has to act |
|---|---|---|
| **Usage-based** | PAP (CPAP and BiPAP) | The patient, plus their doctor for the follow-up visit |
| **Documentation-based** | Oxygen, ventilator, low air loss mattress | The patient's doctor |

**It is not a one-time check.** Equipment that passed at delivery can fall out of compliance later, and the consequences escalate.

| Stage | What it means | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Non-compliant | A lapse — new, or not yet past the grace period | We cannot provide supplies or services for that equipment until it's resolved. There's a **grace period of about one month** after a lapse, when a courtesy delivery is possible unless the notes say otherwise |
| Persistently non-compliant | More than one month past the grace period, after the patient has been told several times | A pick-up ticket may follow |

**How long compliance lasts.** Compliance is confirmed by the compliance team once up-to-date clinical notes (from within the last 6 months) are received, and it's noted with an end date — for example, *compliant until October 2027*. Check the notes for that date.

Backup and urgent escalation contacts are in [[§B-1]].

### §3-12.2 What each item requires

This is the one compliance table in the manual — other chapters point here.

| Item | Type | Requirement | If not met | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen concentrators | Documentation | Periodic updated medical documentation from the provider, plus a current SWO | Supplies and services stop until documentation is received; persistent non-compliance may lead to a pick-up — [[§3-12.1]] | [[§8-8.2]] |
| PAP devices | Usage | ≥ 4 hrs/night on 70% of nights in any consecutive 30-day period inside the first 90, **and** a face-to-face re-evaluation between day 31 and day 91 | Medicare and Medicare Advantage stop paying. Requalifying needs a new in-person evaluation **and** a sleep-lab study — a home test doesn't count. Medicaid and commercial plans set their own requirements | [[§3-7.4]] |
| Ventilators | Documentation | Ongoing physician documentation of continued necessity | Same as oxygen. **Highest priority** — escalate a hold the same day | [[§3-10]] |
| Low air loss mattress | Documentation | **Monthly** clinician documentation that the qualifying wounds are being actively treated | Rental stops and the mattress is picked up. Coverage ends when the wounds heal | [[§2-3.4]] |
