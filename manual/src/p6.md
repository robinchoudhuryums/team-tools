> **About this draft.** Written for **CSRs**. Service owns what happens after delivery — equipment faults, repairs, instruction, and re-education. The question this part answers is when a call belongs to Service rather than somewhere else, and what a CSR can tell the patient before transferring.

## §6-3 Who pays for a repair

The most common Service question a CSR gets, and the answer depends on which of three regimes the equipment is in.

| Situation | Who pays | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Still in the rental period** | UMS owns the equipment and is responsible for maintenance and repairs **at no charge** | [[§10-13.1]] |
| **Patient-owned — month 14 or later** | Medicare covers medically necessary repairs and parts. Deductible and coinsurance apply | [[§10-15]] |
| **Under manufacturer warranty** | Warranty claim, subject to approval and voiding conditions | [[§6-4]] |

> **Watch-out — check the rental status before saying anything about cost.** Telling a rental patient they owe money for a repair is wrong, and telling a patient-owned caller the repair is free is a promise Billing has to walk back. If you can't tell which applies, transfer to Service rather than guessing.

> **Script — patient asking what a repair will cost.** "Let me check how your equipment is set up on the account before I give you a number — it depends on where you are in the rental period and whether it's still under warranty. I'd rather get that right than guess."

> **Policy — no trip charge during a repair.** A patient cannot be charged a service charge, a curb-side fee or a delivery charge while their equipment is being repaired. The one exception is a diagnostic visit that finds nothing needs doing — see [[§6-3.2]].

### §6-3.1 Where the table doesn't apply

Some categories sit outside the ordinary repair-payment rules. For these, "patient-owned, Medicare covers repairs" is the wrong answer.

| Category | What actually applies |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen equipment** | Repairs are not separately reimbursed. Servicing runs on a maintenance-and-servicing payment every 6 months instead — [[§7-8.1]] |
| **Frequent and substantial servicing items** — ventilators, E0471 | Not separately reimbursed. The item is never owned, and servicing is part of the monthly rental — [[§10-13.1]] |
| **Capped rental items during the rental period** | Not separately reimbursed. UMS is responsible |
| **Anything under warranty** | Parts and labour covered by a manufacturer's or supplier's warranty cannot be billed to the patient or to Medicare |

### §6-3.2 Diagnostic visits

A technician can be sent to evaluate equipment, but technicians are normally dispatched to perform a **confirmed** service or repair.

> **Policy — $75 for a diagnostic-only visit.** If a patient requests an in-person visit and no service or repair turns out to be necessary, there is a **$75 out-of-pocket charge** for the visit. If the technician finds and performs a repair, the charge does not apply.

> **Watch-out — say this before the visit is booked, not after.** A patient who learns about the charge when the invoice arrives has a legitimate complaint. Explain that if something is found and fixed there's no charge, and the $75 applies only if the equipment turns out to be working.

> **Script.** "I can get a technician out to look at it. One thing to know first — if they find something and repair it, there's no charge for the visit. If it turns out the equipment is working as it should, there's a $75 charge for the trip. Would you like me to go ahead?"

## §6-4 Manufacturer warranty

| Detail | |
|---|---|
| Typical period | **1 year** in most cases |
| Variation | Some items are shorter or longer — 6 months, 2 years |
| Process | Service **requests** warranty coverage, and the **manufacturer** decides whether it applies |
| Voiding | Certain conditions void a warranty. Water damage is the common one — a PWC left out in the rain likely cannot be serviced under warranty |

Service submits warranty claims and requests **to the manufacturer**. A CSR's role is to route the call, not to assess whether the warranty applies.

## §6-5 Respiratory equipment

### §6-5.1 Ventilators — highest priority

> **Critical.** A patient on a ventilator is on life support. **Any issue involving a vent patient is handled with the highest priority and urgency.**
>
> Service requests go through the Service Q. If unavailable, email **both** `respiratory@universalmedsupply.com` **and** `service@universalmedsupply.com`. See [[§3-10]].
>
> After hours, a ventilator issue means **dispatching the on-call RT immediately** — don't troubleshoot. See [[§8-3]].

### §6-5.2 What Service handles and what stays with CSR

> **Policy** — Complex respiratory service requests that previously went to respiratory therapists exclusively now route through the Service Q. **Oxygen concentrator servicing remains with CSR.**

The Service team may proactively contact patients about concentrator servicing and initiate it, but a patient calling back about that is not a handoff — concentrator servicing is still CSR work. See [[§7-6]].

Trilogy and cough assist issues go to the Service Q, or to `[ROLE: Respiratory Therapists]` directly as a backup.

### §6-5.3 CPAP set-up and instruction

Transfer to Service so they can create the ticket. Field Ops then schedules a respiratory therapist.

## §6-6 Power mobility service

A patient **reporting a problem** with their PMD comes to the Service Q. Once Service has arranged the repair, the visit itself is scheduled through the Field Ops-Power Q — see [[§5-1.1]].

### §6-6.1 Chargers and batteries

UMS stocks some PMD chargers and batteries in the office warehouse, available for out-of-pocket purchase depending on the model.

> **Policy — this is available to non-customers too.** If a caller has no account with us and did not receive their PMD from us, Service may still be able to quote an out-of-pocket cost for a replacement charger or battery, **provided it is a PMD model we carry**.

Don't turn these callers away on the grounds that they aren't our patient. Transfer to the Service Q to check the model and availability.

### §6-6.2 Loaner equipment

> **Policy — don't raise loaners unprompted.** Only discuss a loaner if the caller asks for one.

If they do ask, it depends on why they're without equipment:

| Situation | Loaner |
|---|---|
| **Patient-owned equipment is being repaired** | Possibly. Service must confirm |
| **Still in the rental period** | No separate loaner — UMS is already responsible for the repair |
| **Waiting for a new device to be approved or delivered** | No |

> **Watch-out — don't promise one.** Even where a loaner may be possible, tell the caller you're checking with Service, not that one is coming.

> **Policy — understand the reason before creating a pick-up ticket.** PMD pick-ups are more complicated than other equipment, so the reason matters. Discomfort and faults both come to Service — the full table is in [[§5-9.2]].

## §6-7 Equipment swap-outs

When a patient needs to exchange what they received for a different version of the item, the route depends on **one question: does the change require additional documentation from the MDO?** — a different or additional HCPCS code, or a new MRx, for example.

{{diagram:swap-out}}

### §6-7.1 No additional documentation required

Transfer to Service. They create a **service ticket for the swap-out** and handle it from there. Nothing else is needed.

### §6-7.2 Additional documentation required

This is a coordinated two-sided process, and you start both sides.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Create a pick-up ticket** for the item the patient already received |
| 2 | **Note on the ticket** that it was created for a swap-out, and that pick-up must be **coordinated with the processing and delivery of the new item** |
| 3 | **Email the relevant intake department** explaining the swap-out, so they can request the updated documents from the MDO and initiate the new order |

> **Watch-out — the email is not optional.** The pick-up ticket alone does not start the new order. Without the email, the intake team has no trigger to request the updated documentation, and the patient ends up with their equipment collected and nothing replacing it.

> **Watch-out — do not let the pick-up run ahead of the delivery.** The coordination note exists so the patient isn't left without equipment while the new order waits on documentation. Say so explicitly on the ticket.

> **Script — patient asking to swap an item.** "Let me look at what you have and what you're asking for. If it's a straightforward exchange we can arrange that directly. If the change needs updated paperwork from your doctor's office, we'll get that started and make sure the swap is coordinated so you're not without your equipment in between."

## §6-9 Hospital bed relocation

These calls often arrive as Service calls, but the rule is in [[§5-10]]. The short version:

- Generally **not** done
- Possible exceptions in the **Dallas, San Antonio and Hawaii** areas, moving within the same area
- The bed must still be a **rental** — a patient-owned bed is not ours to move
- **$150**, and subject to supervisor verification

**Who decides.** Either a Field Ops supervisor or a Service supervisor can make the determination and act on it. **If the call comes to a CSR, route it to Service.**

If it's approved, the path is:

**Service ticket created → validated by the Service team → pushed to Field Ops for scheduling**

> **Watch-out** — There are two approvals and two teams in this path, so it is not a same-day answer. Tell the caller it's being evaluated, not that it's being arranged.

## §6-10 Lost, stolen, and irreparably damaged equipment

Medicare will not pay for replacement equipment within the five-year reasonable useful lifetime **unless the item was lost, stolen, or irreparably damaged** — see [[§10-13.5]]. Those are the three narrow exceptions, and they're handled differently.

### §6-10.1 Irreparable damage

Two separate questions get confused here, and they have different answers.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Can the manufacturer cover it?** | Usually no. Irreparable damage is typically **not** a manufacturer defect, and the damage itself has often voided the warranty — water damage and outdoor use being the common causes |
| **Can insurance cover a replacement?** | Possibly — if the damage came from a **specific accident or a natural disaster**, such as a fire or a flood. That's what Medicare means by irreparable damage, and it's one of the three SOS exceptions |

Damage from neglect or misuse isn't irreparable damage in that sense. A PWC left out in the rain has usually voided its warranty **and** doesn't qualify for a covered replacement — it's generally the patient's responsibility ([[§6-10.2]]). A chair destroyed in a house fire is the kind of case the exception is for.

> **Note — the incident has to be documented.** Medicare expects records describing what happened — a police report, an insurance claim report, or a statement from the patient.

> **Watch-out** — Don't tell a patient either answer on the call. Whether damage is irreparable is Service's determination, and whether that produces a covered replacement is Billing's. What a CSR can say is that Service will assess it.

### §6-10.2 Lost or stolen equipment

> **Policy — a police report is required.** Where equipment is lost or damaged **through no fault of the patient**, insurance may agree to pay for a replacement. To initiate that process, UMS requires **a copy of a filed police report** covering the incident.

Without the report, the process doesn't start. This is the first thing to establish on the call.

> **Watch-out — the report is the entry requirement, not the outcome.** Filing a report doesn't guarantee a covered replacement; it's what allows the request to be submitted. Don't tell a patient that a police report means they'll get a new device.

> **Script — patient says their equipment was stolen.** "I'm sorry that happened. Insurance will sometimes cover a replacement when equipment is lost or stolen through no fault of your own, but to start that process we'd need a copy of a police report for the incident. If you haven't filed one yet, that's the first step. Once you have it, send it over and our team can look at what's possible."

> **Note — fault matters.** The "no fault of the patient" condition is what separates this from [[§6-10.1]]. Equipment damaged by water or outdoor use is generally the patient's responsibility and has usually voided the warranty as well.

## §6-11 How long a service request takes

> **Policy — turnaround varies widely and should not be quoted as a range.** Smaller issues may be completed within a few days. Requests needing **parts ordered from the manufacturer** may take several weeks, and logistical delays at that stage are outside UMS's control.

> **Script — patient asking how long.** "It depends on what the technician finds. Straightforward issues are often handled within a few days. If a part has to be ordered from the manufacturer it can take longer — sometimes several weeks — and that part of the timeline isn't in our hands. Service will be in touch once they know what's needed."

This is also why the internal notes rule below matters: a tracking date is not a service date.

## §6-12 Reading Service notes

Notes open from the **Messages** button at the bottom right of the Transaction Workflow window.

> **Policy — "(For internal use only)".** The Service team marks some notes this way. **That information is not relayed to the patient.**

The clearest example: a tracking number for a repair part may be shipping to a **warehouse**, not the patient's home. Service timing therefore won't match what the tracking suggests, because the part still has to be verified and a technician scheduled.

> **Watch-out** — Reading a tracking date off an internal note and giving it to a patient creates a delivery promise nobody made. Service verifies parts and schedules by technician availability.

## §6-15 FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|

| The tracking says my part arrives tomorrow, so why isn't my repair scheduled? | Parts often ship to our warehouse rather than the patient's home. Service verifies the part and schedules by technician availability. See [[§6-12]] |
