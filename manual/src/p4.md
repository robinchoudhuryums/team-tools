## §4-1 Scope and process

Power Mobility covers power wheelchairs (PWC) and power operated vehicles (POV, commonly called scooters). Manual wheelchairs are in [[§2-4]].

### §4-1.1 The Power process

**Sales → Power (PAK → Qualifications → PAR) → Field Ops-Power**

{{diagram:power-process}}

| Phase | What happens | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--scr-ink)"></span>Sales and eligibility | Insurance confirmed, evaluation booked. Moves on at **Eligible** | [[§4-2]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p4)"></span>1 · Intake and education — PAK | PPD interview, MA Education, appointment. Ends when the F2F and PAK are received | [[§4-3]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p9)"></span>2 · Qualifications | Documents reviewed and requested — with PT and ATP evaluations **alongside, if needed** | [[§4-4]] · [[§4-5]] · [[§4-6]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p7)"></span>3 · Verification & authorization | Order verification **any time before ordering**, then PAR packet prep and submission. Appeals if denied | [[§4-7]] · [[§4-8]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p5)"></span>4 · Delivery (Field Ops Power) | Manufacturer order, 2–3 weeks to the warehouse, scheduling, delivery | [[§4-9]] |

An order enters Power once Sales confirms insurance compatibility and an appointment with a qualified provider is set. It leaves Power once authorization approval is received and it is pushed to Field Ops-Power.

### §4-1.2 Timeline expectations

The national average turnaround for a PWC is about 90 days, and UMS is typically faster. Actual times vary widely — under 30 days to over a year — depending on provider responsiveness, insurance type, and patient circumstances.

> **Note — using the 90 days.** Quoting the 90-day national turnaround alongside our faster average can help set accurate expectations for a caller who expects the order to be done in a few days. Then give the current stage and what it's waiting on — a patient told "90 days" at day 200 has been given a number, not an answer.

## §4-2 Insurance eligibility

The Insurance Eligibility team is a division of Sales. They confirm which category of Power Mobility Device, if any, the patient's insurance will cover. Some plans cover a PWC but not a POV, or the reverse.

**Reference:** the insurance lookup in Reference (the app's searchable version of the Payor/HCPCS Order Acceptance sheet), for PMD and scooter acceptance.

### §4-2.1 Eligibility running alongside intake

Two situations may re-involve the Sales Eligibility team on an order already past the Sales stage:

1. The patient is changing insurance mid-order
2. The patient has moved, or the contract or policy governing DME coverage has changed

### §4-2.2 How orders reach Power

| Path | Condition | Route |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Future appointment set and verified with the MDO | Sales pushes the order to a PPD team member, officially moving it to Power intake; the standard PAK process begins |
| 2 | Mobility Evaluation already completed (for example, the patient switched suppliers, or the provider knows the process) | If evidence the F2F was for a PMD Evaluation is confirmed, the paperwork goes straight to Qualifications, skipping PAK. See [[§4-4.3]] |

The appointment date is on the `Transaction` tab, or confirmed in notes.

### §4-2.3 Escalation

Direct transfer to `[ROLE: Insurance Eligibility — Sales]`.

> **Note** — Where someone on the Sales Eligibility team needs to be contacted, go to the lead where possible rather than other team members.

If the lead is unavailable, email `saleseligibility@universalmedsupply.com` with the transaction, the insurance being changed to, and the effective date. Tell the patient we'll update them shortly.

## §4-3 PAK (Pre-Appointment Kit)

The PAK stage is about understanding the patient's mobility needs, preparing the packet of documents that is sent to the MDO before the upcoming Mobility Evaluation appointment, and making sure the MDO understands what needs to be done.

### §4-3.1 PPD (Patient Provided Data)

The first stage of the Power process. Once eligibility is confirmed and an appointment set, Sales assigns the order to a PPD team member for a roughly 15-minute PPD Interview covering the patient's mobility issues, PMD options, and the process overall.

`[FIGURE: PPD note template — no image in the sanitized export]`

If the order clears this step, the team member creates the PAK documents and sends them to the MDO for the appointment date. MA Education then contacts the MDO to confirm the PAK was received, and to discuss those documents with the doctor, Medical Assistant, or Nurse.

After the PPD, the PAK team sends the patient a link to an optional consent form and AOR form, used for an appeal if the order is later denied.

> **Watch-out — the Seating Evaluation mistake.** The PAK includes a pre-filled SE watermarked "Patient Provided Data" as a reference guideline (based on the patient's PPD responses and insurance guidelines), followed by two blank SE pages for the provider to complete from their own assessment. MDOs frequently return the pre-filled pages instead of completing the blank ones, so it is worth clarifying how that is meant to be completed.

### §4-3.2 MA Education

After the PAK documents are sent, MA Education contacts the MDO about them — see [[§4-3.1]].

`[FIGURE: MA Education note template — no image in the sanitized export]`

**When the MA Education team is unavailable**

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Several days until the appointment | Tell the MDO an associate will call back to go over the paperwork |
| Appointment is same-day, no MA Education available | Advise the MDO directly — see the four points below |

For a same-day appointment, tell the MDO:

1. Complete the paperwork per the written instructions during the visit
2. The purpose of the visit must be a **Power Mobility Device Evaluation**
3. SE pages watermarked "Patient Provided Data" are reference only — complete the blank copies
4. Complete and return the documents, or give us an update as soon as they are able
5. If the provider decides to refer the patient to a PT/OT for this, that's fine — but we'll then need a **PT/OT Eval Rx** for the insurance. We can help the patient through that process with whichever PT/OT they're referred to or already use, or help them find an eligible local service. See [[§4-5]]

### §4-3.3 New appointment

Involved only when the patient needs to reschedule the Mobility Evaluation. Common reasons:

- The original appointment wasn't specifically for a PMD Evaluation
- The F2F is over 6 months old
- The provider says they or the patient don't qualify

Ask whether the patient has made a new appointment. If yes, note the date, time, and provider. If no, explain why it's needed, and advise they schedule the appointment and call us back with the details. Contact one of the PAK Appointment Scheduling sub-team members — `[ROLE: PAK Appointment Scheduling]` — if further assistance is needed.

### §4-3.4 Appointment passed

When the Mobility Evaluation date has passed but the F2F and PAK documents haven't been returned, this sub-team contacts the MDO and the patient to confirm the appointment took place, and requests the pending paperwork if so.

> **Note** — If the note reads "Trying to Obtain After Visit Documents," confirm the visit was completed specifically for a PMD Evaluation. We are most likely requesting the F2F and the completed PAK documents. Confirm the fax number and re-fax if needed.

Once the PAK documents and F2F are confirmed received, the order is pushed to Qualifications.

## §4-4 Qualifications

Once the F2F and PAK documents arrive, the Qualifications team reviews them and requests corrections or additional information per insurance guidelines. MDOs can often be unresponsive, so this team follows up regularly until all of the necessary documents are received.

### §4-4.1 Status update calls

Most Power calls are status updates, and most of those are about documentation we've requested from the MDO.

**Always check:** open the `Transaction` tab and click **Fax History**, then open the most recent fax sent. Compare the documentation request against the most recent artifacts received, and explain any obvious discrepancy — a missing signature, the wrong provider, an incomplete form.

`[FIGURE: Qualifications notation template showing remaining documents needed]`
`[FIGURE: Documentation request example — no image in the sanitized export]`

### §4-4.2 Documentation request older than 3 days

In order:

1. Confirm the fax number and ask for a backup number, then re-fax
2. If the MDO is hard to reach, ask whether they can receive it by secure email — if so, you must enable Virtru protection
3. Last resort: email the request to the patient, or have a Power team member physically mail the documents to the patient to bring to the MDO in person

### §4-4.3 Qualified Lead (QL)

A patient who has already completed a valid Mobility Evaluation is a Qualified Lead. Once Sales Eligibility confirms eligibility, the order goes directly to `[ROLE: Qualified Leads]`, skipping the PAK stage. **A PPD is still conducted** — directly by a member of the Qualifications team (T3Q, `[ROLE: T3Q (Qualifications sub-team)]`) rather than the PAK stage's PPD sub-team — and the order continues through Qualifications.

If the Mobility Evaluation is scheduled for a future date, the order follows the standard PAK process instead.

## §4-5 PT Evaluation

A Physical Therapy Evaluation isn't required on every order, but may be needed per insurance guidelines or the doctor's recommendation. The PT Eval team schedules evaluations with local qualifying practices.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| PT/OT office has specific questions about the appointment or documents | Direct transfer to `[ROLE: PT Evaluation Team]`. If unavailable, email `power@universalmedsupply.com` — subject `PT Eval: [Patient name] [TRX #]`, per [[§0-11.2]] |
| Patient calling for status, PT Eval needed | Check whether the PT Rx has been received. If not, it is needed from the MDO — the patient will be contacted to schedule once the PT/OT Rx arrives. Note whether the patient already sees a physical therapist, receives Home Health (HH) services, or is on hospice; if so, include the company's name and contact information |
| PT Rx confirmed received | Connect the patient directly with the PT Eval team to schedule |
| PT/OT office asks for an ATP to attend | Not always possible in person. The PT Eval team may be able to arrange virtual attendance, or coordinate with Field Ops-Power to check whether in-person attendance is feasible |
| Provider refers the patient to PT/OT instead of completing the ME | Explain that a PT Rx and the F2F from that visit are still needed, and that we'll later send a PT Report Concurrence request for the provider to sign after the PT/OT Eval is completed |
| Waiting on the PT report | The team follows up with the PT/OT office. The patient can call the practice to expedite — you can give them the practice name and phone number from the notes |

### §4-5.1 PT Report Concurrence

The provider's sign-off on the PT/OT findings. The signature section to complete is often at the bottom of the last page.

## §4-6 ATP Evaluation

An ATP (Assistive Technology Professional) Evaluation is often required per insurance guidelines. A qualified evaluator (ATP) visits the patient at home to confirm the environment can accommodate the equipment, and that the right equipment has been selected for the patient's particular needs. It includes measurements and photos of doorways, entrances, and even the patient themselves.

`[FIGURE: ATP scheduling notation — no image in the sanitized export]`

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Patient calling for ATP status, no recent contact noted | Field Ops-Power will contact them to schedule — up to 2–3 weeks out, depending on the ATP's travel route |
| Note says "ATP evaluation marked as requested" | One will be necessary. No transfer needed — Field Ops-Power will call when they can schedule |
| Patient in a remote or rural area | A virtual ATP evaluation may be acceptable. Virtual evaluations are scheduled only by `[ROLE: Virtual ATP Scheduling]` — in-person visits, the usual case, are scheduled by Field Ops-Power |
| **ATP scheduling was unsuccessful and noted as a "drive-by" for a given date** | **Still transfer to Field Ops-Power for proper scheduling if the patient calls** |
| ATP evaluation completed | The ATP uploads the ATP Report after the evaluation |

> **Note** — There are a limited number of these specially trained ATPs, and they travel long distances all around the country, which can mean longer scheduling timeframes. They can only schedule a visit when one is, or will be, in the area.

## §4-7 Prior Authorization

At the PAR stage all collected information is organized and submitted to insurance for review. The goal is approval for every requested HCPCS code so the equipment can be delivered and paid for.

`[FIGURE: PAR submission notation example]`

### §4-7.1 Status update calls at the PAR stage

| Status | What to tell the caller |
|---|---|
| PAR submitted, not yet returned | We're pending authorization. Insurers typically take about 14 days to review and send the decision to both us and the patient |
| PAR returned — approval received | If it was fully approved, the next step will be ordering with the manufacturer — 2–3 weeks to our warehouse, then delivery is scheduled |
| PAR returned — denial received | If the patient is aware of the denial, advise that the Authorization team will analyze the denial letter for a possible appeal. If they aren't aware, once the denial is confirmed in the notes you can say: "It appears we received a denial from your insurance; our team is reviewing the response in detail for a possible appeal." |

> **Watch-out — check order verification on an approval or a pending decision.** For the first two rows, check whether Order Verification ([[§4-8]]) has already been completed or is pending. If it's pending, transfer to Field Ops-Power to complete it.

### §4-7.2 Calls from insurance representatives

| Insurance is… | Action |
|---|---|
| Providing an approval or denial decision | Follow [[§0-12.3]] — record the decision and reference number, confirm the letter will be faxed (or emailed if possible), post your notes, and email the Power intake team — subject `PAR Response Received: [Patient name] [TRX #]` |
| Requesting additional information | Transfer to the Authorization team — `[ROLE: Power Authorization (PAR) Team]`. If unavailable, email `power@universalmedsupply.com` — subject `PAR Additional Info Requested: [Patient name] [TRX #]`, per [[§0-11.2]] — including the required information and the deadline |

> **Policy — dual submission.** Some PARs are submitted to both traditional Medicare and the patient's Medicare Advantage plan — the notes say **dual submission**. If the MA plan denies but Medicare approves, Medicare's approval is sent to the MA plan to support the case for approval. This can potentially add 2 weeks or more to processing time.

### §4-7.3 Appeals

On a denial, the insurance's response letter is reviewed for evidence supporting an appeal. Appeals can generally take up to 1–2 months. If the appeal(s) are also denied, the order will be closed.

| Caller | Action |
|---|---|
| Insurance calling about an appeal | Transfer to an Authorization Appeal specialist — `[ROLE: Prior Authorization Specialist — Complex PWC / Appeals]`, who submits appeals. If unavailable, email Power with the subject `Appeal: [Patient name] [TRX #]` |
| Patient asking for status after a denial | We've received a response and it's under review. If an appeal is possible, the Authorization team will submit it to the insurance, and we will contact you once we get their response |

## §4-8 Order Verification

Field Ops-Power calls the patient to confirm order details before the equipment is ordered. This prevents rejection at delivery and post-delivery dissatisfaction.

Finalized on this call: make and model, seat size, joystick side, and color. An image or brochure is sent to the patient for confirmation.

`[FIGURE: Completed PWC Verification note — no image in the sanitized export]`

> **Note — timing.** Verification usually happens after any PT or ATP evaluation (as these may affect the model or HCPCS code selection), and can overlap Qualifications or PAR — it's often done while authorization is pending, so the order can be placed immediately on approval. It must be complete before the order goes to the manufacturer.

## §4-9 Delivery and returns

Once the order is verified and approved, Field Ops-Power places it with the manufacturer. Typical ETA to our warehouse is 2–3 weeks, sometimes shorter.

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Technician delivery | Trained technicians deliver, educate the user on functionality, and adjust as needed. Re-education can be arranged through the Service team |
| Where it's delivered | The patient's home, and the patient **must be home**.[^facility] See [[§5-4.4]] |
| Reporting issues | Report any issue as soon as possible — the sooner we know, the easier it is to fix. Who pays for a repair depends on rental status and warranty. See [[§6-3]] |
| Rejecting delivery | The patient can reject the delivery if unsatisfied. Saying so can reassure a patient who is hesitant to proceed for fear of getting something they don't like with no recourse |
| Delivery tickets | Every delivery includes a signed acknowledgment from both technician and patient |
| Freight company calls | Ask for the freight company's name and the city they're delivering to, then transfer to Field Ops-Power, relaying both. See [[§5-14]] |
| Route date vs. ticket date | These may differ — PMD routes are scheduled within date ranges because technicians cover large areas |

[^facility]: A PMD can't be delivered to a hospital, rehab facility or SNF, but it can be delivered to a nursing home that is the patient's permanent residence.

### §4-9.1 Returns

> **Policy — PWC returns can be trickier than other item categories.** Power wheelchairs are custom-built to each patient's measurements and cannot be reissued to another patient, so it may not be possible to pick one up.
>
> That doesn't mean refusing the request. Once the preceding resolution checks in [[§5-9.2]] are attempted — no servicing solution, no affordability issue — **create the pick-up ticket**. Whether the chair is collected or written off is decided afterward, so don't guarantee either.

| Billing type | What happens |
|---|---|
| Billed as purchase | If a one-time payment has been received from the insurance (check the `Claim Prep` tab — [[§10-20.5]]), it likely will not be picked up — the patient owns it and may donate or dispose of it |
| Billed as rent-to-own | If we received payment for only some of the months in the 13-month rental period, the remaining balance may be waived rather than the equipment picked up — a return can mean the insurance expects to be refunded, so accepting the loss may be the better outcome. Field Ops decides — [[§10-15.1]] |

The billing treatment behind this is in [[§10-13]].

### §4-10.3 Standard and complex rehab power wheelchairs

Medicare's power wheelchair policy groups chairs by performance and options, and the grouping decides what an order needs:

| Type | Medicare groups | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Group 2 with no power options | The standard process; billed as a rental only |
| **Complex rehab** | Group 2 with single or multiple power options, and Group 3 and up | A specialty evaluation (PT/OT or physician) and an ATP's involvement in selecting the chair. It can also be billed as a purchase at delivery |

The model's group is in the Classification column above.
