## §4-1 Scope and process

Power Mobility covers power wheelchairs (PWC) and power operated vehicles (POV, commonly called scooters). Manual wheelchairs are in [[§2-4]].

### §4-1.1 The Power process

**Sales → Power (PAK → Qualifications → PAR) → Field Ops-Power**

{{diagram:power-process}}

| Phase | What happens | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--scr-ink)"></span>Sales and eligibility | Insurance confirmed, evaluation booked. Moves on at **Eligible** | [[§4-2]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p4)"></span>1 · Intake and education — PAK | PPD interview, MA Education, appointment. Ends when the F2F and PAK are received | [[§4-3]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p9)"></span>2 · Qualifications | Documents reviewed and requested — with PT and ATP evaluations **alongside, if needed** | [[§4-4]] · [[§4-5]] · [[§4-6]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p7)"></span>3 · Verification & authorization | Order verification **any time before ordering**, then PAR packet prep and submission. Appeals if denied | [[§4-7]] · [[§4-8]] |
| <span class="phase" style="background:var(--p8)"></span>4 · Delivery (Field Ops Power) | Manufacturer order, 2–3 weeks to the warehouse, scheduling, delivery | [[§4-9]] |

An order enters Power once Sales confirms insurance compatibility and an appointment with a qualified provider is set. It leaves Power once authorization approval is received and it is pushed to Field Ops-Power.

### §4-1.2 Timeline expectations

The national average turnaround for a PWC is about 90 days, and UMS is typically faster. Actual times vary widely — under 30 days to over a year — depending on provider responsiveness, insurance type, and patient circumstances.

> **Watch-out** — Don't quote 90 days as an estimate for a specific order. Give the caller the current stage and what it's waiting on. A patient told "90 days" at day 200 has been given a number, not an answer.

## §4-2 Insurance eligibility

The Insurance Eligibility team is a division of Sales. They confirm which category of Power Mobility Device, if any, the patient's insurance will cover. Some plans cover a PWC but not a POV, or the reverse.

**Reference:** the Payor/HCPCS Order Acceptance sheet, for PMD and scooter acceptance regions.

Where eligibility is unclear on a new order, advise sending a referral for review.

### §4-2.1 Eligibility running alongside intake

Two situations put eligibility back in play on an order already in progress:

1. The patient is changing insurance mid-order
2. The patient has moved, or the contract or policy governing DME coverage has changed

### §4-2.2 How orders reach Power

| Path | Condition | Route |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Future appointment set and verified with the MDO | PPD team contacts the patient; standard PAK process begins |
| 2 | Mobility Evaluation already completed — patient switched suppliers, or the provider knows the process | Paperwork goes straight to Qualifications, skipping PAK. See [[§4-4.3]] |

The appointment date is on the `Transaction` tab, or confirmed in notes.

### §4-2.3 Escalation

Direct transfer to `[ROLE: Insurance Eligibility — Sales]`.

> **Note** — Where someone on the Sales Eligibility team needs to be contacted, go to the lead where possible rather than other team members.

If the lead is unavailable, email `saleseligibility@universalmedsupply.com` with the transaction, the insurance being changed to, and the effective date. Tell the patient we'll update them shortly.

## §4-3 PAK (Pre-Appointment Kit)

The PAK stage is about completing and receiving the paperwork for the Mobility Evaluation. That appointment with a qualified provider is what secures insurance approval.

### §4-3.1 PPD (Patient Provided Data)

The first stage of the Power process. Once eligibility is confirmed and an appointment set, Sales assigns the order to a PPD team member for a roughly 15-minute PPD Interview covering the patient's mobility issues, PMD options, and the process overall.

`[FIGURE: PPD note template — no image in the sanitized export]`

If the order clears this step, the team member creates the PAK documents and sends them to the MDO for the appointment date. MA Education contacts the MDO in parallel.

> **Policy — consent and AOR forms.** After the PPD is completed, the PAK team sends the patient a link to a HIPAA-compliant consent form and an AOR form. Neither is mandatory. They are used for appeal purposes if the order is later denied.

> **Watch-out — the Seating Evaluation mistake.** The PAK includes a pre-filled SE watermarked "Patient Provided Data" as a reference guideline, followed by two blank SE pages for the provider to complete from their own assessment. MDOs frequently return the pre-filled pages instead of completing the blank ones.

### §4-3.2 MA Education

After the PAK documents are sent, the MA Education team contacts the MDO to confirm receipt and walk a medical assistant or nurse through the paperwork.

`[FIGURE: MA Education note template — no image in the sanitized export]`

**When the MA Education team is unavailable**

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Several days until the appointment | Tell the MDO an associate will call back to go over the paperwork |
| Appointment is same-day, no MA Education available | Advise the MDO directly — see the four points below |

For a same-day appointment, tell the MDO:

1. Complete the paperwork per the written instructions during the visit
2. The purpose of the visit must be a **Power Mobility Device Evaluation**
3. SE pages watermarked "Patient Provided Data" are reference only — complete the blank copies
4. Call back, or we will email for follow-up

### §4-3.3 New appointment

Involved only when the patient needs to reschedule the Mobility Evaluation. Common reasons:

- The original appointment wasn't specifically for a PMD Evaluation
- The F2F is over 6 months old
- The provider says they or the patient don't qualify

Ask whether the patient has made a new appointment. If yes, note the date, time, and provider. If no, explain why it's needed and advise scheduling. Contact `[ROLE: PAK / PPD / MA Education Team]` if further help is needed.

### §4-3.4 Appointment passed

When the Mobility Evaluation date has passed but the F2F and PAK documents haven't been returned, this team contacts the MDO to confirm the visit happened and request the paperwork.

> **Watch-out** — If the note reads "Trying to Obtain After Visit Documents," confirm the visit was completed specifically for a PMD Evaluation. We are most likely requesting the F2F and the completed PAK documents. Confirm the fax number and re-fax if needed.

Once the PAK documents and F2F are confirmed received, the order is pushed to Qualifications.

## §4-4 Qualifications

Once the F2F and PAK documents arrive, the Qualifications team reviews them and requests corrections or additional information per insurance guidelines. MDOs are often unresponsive, so this team follows up repeatedly.

### §4-4.1 Status update calls

Most Power calls are status updates, and most of those are about documentation we've requested from the MDO.

**Always check:** open the `Transaction` tab and click **Fax History**, then open the most recent fax sent. Compare the documentation request against the most recent artifacts received, and explain any obvious discrepancy — a missing signature, the wrong provider, an incomplete form.

`[FIGURE: Qualifications notation template showing remaining documents needed]`
`[FIGURE: Documentation request example — no image in the sanitized export]`

### §4-4.2 Documentation request older than 3 days

In order:

1. Confirm the fax number and ask for a backup number, then re-fax
2. If the MDO is hard to reach, ask whether they can receive it by secure email — enable Virtru protection
3. Last resort: email the request to the patient, or have a Power team member mail the documents to the patient to bring to the MDO physically

### §4-4.3 Qualified Lead (QL)

A patient who has already completed a valid Mobility Evaluation is a Qualified Lead. Once Sales Eligibility confirms eligibility, the order goes directly to `[ROLE: Qualified Leads]`, skipping the PAK stage. **A PPD is still conducted** — by a member of the Qualifications team rather than the PAK stage's PPD team — and the order continues through Qualifications.

If the Mobility Evaluation is scheduled for a future date, the order follows the standard PAK process instead.

## §4-5 PT Evaluation

A Physical Therapy Evaluation isn't required on every order, but may be needed per insurance guidelines or the doctor's recommendation. The PT Eval team schedules evaluations with local qualifying practices.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| PT/OT office has specific questions | Direct transfer to `[ROLE: PT Evaluation Team]`. If unavailable, email `power@universalmedsupply.com` — subject `PT Eval: [Patient name] [TRX #]`, per [[§0-11.2]] |
| Patient calling for status, PT Eval needed | Check whether the PT Rx has been received. If not, it's being requested from the MDO — the patient will be contacted to schedule in 1–2 weeks, or once the PT Rx arrives |
| PT Rx confirmed received | Connect the patient directly with the PT Eval team to schedule |
| PT/OT office asks for an ATP to attend | Rarely possible in person. The PT Eval team may arrange virtual attendance; if not, another eligible practice may be needed |
| Provider refers the patient to PT/OT instead of completing the ME | We need the PT Rx and appointment details. The provider must also sign the PT Report Concurrence |
| Waiting on the PT report | The team follows up with the PT/OT office. The patient can call the practice to expedite — give them the practice name and phone from the notes |

## §4-6 ATP Evaluation

An ATP (Assistive Technology Professional) Evaluation is often required per insurance guidelines. A qualified evaluator visits the patient at home to confirm the environment can accommodate the equipment and that the right equipment has been selected. It includes measurements of doorways and entrances, plus photos.

`[FIGURE: ATP scheduling notation — no image in the sanitized export]`

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Patient calling for ATP status, no recent contact noted | Field Ops-Power will contact them to schedule — up to 2–3 weeks out, depending on the ATP's travel route |
| Note says "ATP evaluation marked as requested" | One will be necessary. No transfer needed — Field Ops-Power will call when they can schedule |
| Patient in a remote or rural area | A virtual ATP evaluation may be acceptable. Virtual evaluations are scheduled only by `[ROLE: Virtual ATP Scheduling]` — in-person visits, the usual case, are scheduled by Field Ops-Power |
| **ATP scheduling was unsuccessful and noted as a "drive-by" for a given date** | **Still transfer to Field Ops-Power for proper scheduling if the patient calls** |
| ATP evaluation completed | The ATP uploads the ATP Report after the evaluation |

> **Note** — ATPs are limited in number and travel long distances. They can only schedule a visit when one is, or will be, in the area. The scheduled delivery date and the route dates on the `Ticket` tab may differ, because technicians cover large areas within date ranges.

## §4-7 Prior Authorization

At the PAR stage all collected information is organized and submitted to insurance for review. The goal is approval for every requested HCPCS code so the equipment can be delivered and paid for.

`[FIGURE: PAR submission notation example]`

### §4-7.1 Status update calls at the PAR stage

| Status | What to tell the caller |
|---|---|
| PAR submitted, not yet returned | We're pending authorization. Insurers typically take 14 days or less to review and send the decision to both us and the patient |
| PAR returned — approval received | The response is in and under review. Next step is ordering with the manufacturer — 2–3 weeks to our warehouse, then delivery is scheduled |
| PAR returned — denial received | The response is in and under review. The Authorization team will analyze it for a possible appeal |

### §4-7.2 Calls from insurance representatives

| Insurance is… | Action |
|---|---|
| Providing an approval or denial decision | Follow [[§0-12.3]] — record the decision and reference number, confirm the letter will be faxed or emailed, post your notes, and email the Power intake team |
| Requesting additional information | Transfer to `[ROLE: Prior Authorization Specialist — Standard PWC]` or `[ROLE: Prior Authorization Specialist — Complex PWC / Appeals]`. If unavailable, email `power@universalmedsupply.com` — subject `Authorization Request: [Patient name] [TRX #]`, per [[§0-11.2]] — including the required information and the deadline |

> **Policy — Medicare and Medicare Advantage submission.** Where a PMD order needs to go to both Medicare and a Medicare Advantage plan, the Authorization team either submits to both simultaneously, or submits to Medicare after an MA denial has already been received. This can add two weeks or more to processing, though not always.

> **Watch-out — what patients get wrong about MA.** Patients often call saying they have Medicare with Medicare Advantage as their secondary. **An MA plan is the primary, not a secondary** — it replaces Part B entirely, and claims go to the plan rather than to Medicare. Correct this gently before discussing coverage. See [[§10-8]].

### §4-7.3 Appeals

On a denial, the insurance's response letter is reviewed for evidence supporting an appeal. Appeals generally take up to a month. If the appeals are also denied, the order is closed.

| Caller | Action |
|---|---|
| Insurance calling about an appeal | Transfer to `[ROLE: Prior Authorization Specialist — Complex PWC / Appeals]`, who submits appeals. If unavailable, take a message for a callback |
| Patient asking for status after a denial | We've received a response and it's under review. If an appeal is possible, the Authorization team will initiate it and we'll await the response |

## §4-8 Order Verification

Field Ops-Power calls the patient to confirm order details before the equipment is ordered. This prevents post-delivery dissatisfaction.

Finalized on this call: make and model, seat size, joystick side, and color. An image or brochure is sent to the patient for confirmation.

`[FIGURE: Completed PWC Verification note — no image in the sanitized export]`

> **Note — timing.** Verification usually happens after any PT or ATP evaluation, and can overlap Qualifications or PAR — it's often done while authorization is pending, so the order can be placed immediately on approval. It must be complete before the order goes to the manufacturer.

## §4-9 Delivery and returns

Once the order is verified and approved, Field Ops-Power places it with the manufacturer. Typical ETA to our warehouse is 2–3 weeks, sometimes shorter.

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Technician delivery | Trained technicians deliver, educate the user on functionality, and adjust as needed. Re-education can be arranged through the Service team |
| Where it's delivered | The patient's home — **never a facility** — and the patient **must be home**. See [[§5-4.4]] |
| Reporting issues | Report any issue as soon as possible — the sooner we know, the easier it is to fix. Who pays for a repair depends on rental status and warranty, not time since delivery. See [[§6-3]] |
| Rejecting delivery | The patient can reject the delivery if unsatisfied |
| Delivery tickets | Every delivery includes a signed acknowledgment from both technician and patient |
| Freight company calls | Ask for the freight company's name and the city they're delivering to, then transfer to Field Ops-Power, relaying both. See [[§5-14]] |
| Route date vs. ticket date | These may differ — PMD routes are scheduled within date ranges because of travel distances |

### §4-9.1 Returns

> **Policy — PWCs generally cannot be returned.** Power wheelchairs are custom-built to each patient's measurements and cannot be reissued to another patient, so we generally do not pick them up.
>
> That doesn't mean refusing the request. Once the checks in [[§5-9.2]] are clear — no repair, no affordability issue — **create the pick-up ticket**. Whether the chair is collected or written off is decided afterward. Don't promise either.

| Billing type | What happens |
|---|---|
| Billed as purchase | The patient owns the equipment. It cannot be picked up. They may donate or dispose of it |
| Billed as rent-to-own | We may have received only partial payment. The company may write off the remaining balance rather than pick up, because a return means insurance expects full payment back — accepting the loss is often the better outcome |

The billing treatment behind this is in [[§10-13]].
