## §10-1 2026 annual figures

> **Short version.** The 2026 Part B deductible is $283; coinsurance is 20% of the Medicare-approved amount. Traditional (Original) Medicare has **no** out-of-pocket maximum. Medicare Advantage does.

| Item | 2026 amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Medicare Part B annual deductible | $283 | Resets January 1. Applies to all DME billed to traditional Medicare |
| Medicare Part B coinsurance | 20% of the Medicare-approved amount | After the deductible. No annual out-of-pocket maximum |
| Medicare Advantage in-network MOOP (maximum) | $9,250 | Plan-specific; many plans set a lower MOOP |
| Medigap high-deductible Plans F & G | $2,950 annual deductible | Only for patients who chose a high-deductible version |

CMS publishes the following year's amounts in October–November. Update this table and the build version; nothing else changes.

## §10-2 Billing types and product categories

> **Short version.** Three billing types: **capped rental** (13 months, then the patient owns it), **continuous monthly rental** (never owned), and **purchase** (billed once). Oxygen is its own 36-month case. Know which applies before discussing cost.

| Type | What happens | Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Capped rental** | Monthly claims for 13 months, then ownership transfers automatically | Beds, wheelchairs, power mobility, lifts, CPAP, BiPAP (E0470 and E0471), suction, LALM |
| **Continuous monthly rental** | Monthly claims for as long as the item is needed; ownership never transfers | Ventilators |
| **Oxygen (36-month)** | 36 months of rental, then no rental charge — but the supplier keeps ownership | Oxygen concentrators and cylinders |
| **Purchase** | Billed once | Walkers, rollators, nebulizers, braces, bathroom items, accessories |
| **Purchase (resupply)** | Billed per shipment on a recurring schedule | PAP, trach, suction, catheter, incontinence supplies |

## §10-3 Medicare Part B basics

> **Short version.** We bill Part B. The patient meets the annual deductible first, then Medicare pays 80% and the patient owes 20% — of **Medicare's approved amount** for that item in that area, not our retail price.

| Part | Covers | DME relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Part A — Hospital | Inpatient hospital, SNF, hospice, home health | Not billed by UMS. While a patient is inpatient the facility is responsible for DME and rental billing pauses — [[§10-14]]. Hospice: [[§10-11]] |
| Part B — Medical (incl. DME) | Outpatient, doctor visits, DME, preventive | Primary payer for DME. Also covers nebulizer inhalation medications as DME drugs |
| Part C — Medicare Advantage | Everything in A + B through a private plan | Replaces Part B for billing. Claims go to the plan — [[§10-7]] |
| Part D — Prescription drugs | Pharmacy drugs | Not used for DME. Covers metered-dose inhalers, **not** nebulizer solutions |

> **Note — no out-of-pocket maximum on traditional Medicare.** A patient's 20% coinsurance accumulates without limit. Medicare Advantage plans have a MOOP; traditional Medicare does not.

## §10-4 Deductibles and cost-sharing

> **Short version.** The deductible resets January 1. Until it is met the patient owes 100% of approved charges — doctor visits count toward it too. Five steps to an estimate.

Until the patient has paid the full deductible in Medicare-approved charges for the year — from any Part B provider — they owe 100% of approved charges. After that the 80/20 split applies. Rental items apply the deductible month by month.

{{diagram:cost-share}}

### §10-4.1 Cost estimate in five steps

1. **Payer type?** Traditional Medicare / Medicare Advantage / Medicaid-MCO only / commercial
2. **Traditional Medicare — deductible met?** No → patient pays 100% until met. Yes → Medicare 80%, patient 20%
3. **Secondary?** Medigap (most plans, e.g. Plan G) → covers the 20% — Plan G still owes the Part B deductible. Full Medicaid or QMB → patient owes $0, including the deductible. SLMB/QI or none → patient owes 20%
4. **Add any upgrade fee** — [[§10-15]]
5. **Quote as an estimate** using the language in [[§10-20.2]]

### §10-4.2 Worked examples (2026 deductible $283)

| Situation | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Walker in February, approved $120, no Part B services yet | Patient owes $120, all applied to the deductible. $163 of deductible remains |
| Same patient starts a wheelchair rental, monthly approved $200 | Month 1: $163 finishes the deductible + 20% of the remaining $37 = $170.40. Later months: $40 |
| Deductible already met, semi-electric bed at $250/month | 20% of $250 = $50/month. Medicare pays $200 |

## §10-5 Secondary coverage: Medigap, dual eligibility, QMB

> **Short version.** Medigap covers the 20%. Medicaid and QMB take the patient to $0 on Medicare-covered items. QMB-only is **not** the same as full Medicaid, and SLMB/QI carries no cost-sharing protection at all.

### §10-5.1 Medigap

Plan G (and Plan F for those eligible before 2020) covers the 20% coinsurance after Medicare pays its 80%. Plan G requires the patient to pay the Part B deductible first; Plan F covers it.

A Medigap patient's total annual out-of-pocket for Medicare-covered DME is typically just the Part B deductible. **Upgrade fees are still owed** — Medigap never covers them.

> **Watch-out** — Medigap cannot be combined with Medicare Advantage. If a patient says they have "a supplement" and an MA plan, one of those is a misunderstanding. Usually the "supplement" is the MA plan itself, or a separate dental/vision product.

### §10-5.2 Three kinds of dual eligibility

| Type | What it is | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Medicaid** (QMB Plus / full dual) | The state's full Medicaid benefit package, often through an MCO such as Molina, in addition to Medicare | Covers Medicare cost-sharing **and** may cover items Medicare does not. Medicaid can be billed primary for certain items in enrolled states |
| **QMB only** (partial dual) | A Medicare Savings Program paying premiums and cost-sharing, **without** full Medicaid benefits | Cannot be billed for Medicare cost-sharing. Medicaid does not cover non-Medicare items for them |
| **SLMB / QI** (partial dual) | Pays the Part B premium only | Still owes deductible and coinsurance like any traditional Medicare patient. **No cost-sharing protection** |

### §10-5.3 QMB

> **Policy — federal billing protection.** Providers may **not** bill a QMB patient for any Medicare cost-sharing — deductible, coinsurance, or copay — even if Medicaid pays less than the full amount or pays nothing. Billing a QMB patient for these amounts violates federal law and the Medicare provider agreement.
>
> If a QMB patient receives an invoice for Medicare cost-sharing, it must be corrected and any payment refunded. Route to Billing immediately.

QMB status appears on the eligibility check and on the Medicare remittance. When in doubt, ask Billing before discussing any balance.

**What QMB does not do:** it does not waive upgrade fees other than the standard rollator fee ([[§10-15]]), and it does not make Medicaid cover items Medicare denies.

> **Policy — billing order.** Bill Medicare first, then Medicaid — except a hospital bed for a full-Medicaid patient in a state where UMS is enrolled, where Medicaid is billed primary ([[§10-15.1]]). If the primary does not cover an item, the secondary generally cannot cover the full cost — it covers only remaining cost-sharing on approved items. The exception is items Medicaid covers on its own, such as TX Medicaid incontinence supplies for a full-Medicaid patient.

## §10-6 Coordination of benefits and Medicare Secondary Payer

> **Short version.** An "other insurance is primary" denial usually means the payer's records are stale, and **only the patient can fix it**. Employer coverage can make Medicare secondary.

### §10-6.1 The most common COB problem

A claim denies with "other insurance is primary" or "COB information needed" even though the patient says they have one plan. The payer's COB record is usually stale — an old employer plan never terminated in Medicare's file.

Ask the patient to call the number on their insurance card (traditional Medicare: 1-800-MEDICARE) and confirm their coordination of benefits. Note the account and tell Billing so the claim can be resubmitted (or the relevant intake or eligibility team if the order is still in progress).

### §10-6.2 When Medicare is secondary

| Situation | Who is primary |
|---|---|
| An employer group health plan (EGHP): still working (or a spouse working) at 65+ with an employer of 20+ employees; on Medicare through disability with an employer of 100+; or end-stage renal disease, for the first 30 months | Employer plan — for end-stage renal disease, for the first 30 months, then Medicare |
| DME need results from an accident with liability, no-fault, or workers' compensation coverage | That coverage. Medicare may pay conditionally and recover later |
| Retiree coverage or COBRA plus Medicare | Medicare |

If a patient mentions any of these, note it so verification can complete the MSP questionnaire before the claim is filed.

## §10-7 Medicare Advantage (Part C)

> **Short version.** An MA plan **replaces** Part B. Claims go to the plan. Prior authorization is far more common than under traditional Medicare. No ABN and no Medigap — but QMB and Medicaid still apply.

| Feature | Traditional Medicare | Medicare Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-sharing | 20% after the Part B deductible | Flat copay or coinsurance; some plans have a separate DME deductible |
| Prior authorization | Required for a defined list only — power wheelchairs, Group 2 support surfaces (LALM E0277), certain knee/back braces, lower-limb prosthetics | Frequently required for most DME, including beds, CPAP, oxygen |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | None | Plan MOOP — [[§10-1]] |
| Network | Any Medicare-enrolled supplier | Often restricted; some plans use a single DME vendor |
| Secondary insurance | Medigap allowed | Medigap not allowed. QMB/Medicaid still applies |
| Denial notices | ABN before service — [[§10-17.1]] | No ABN; the plan's own denial/EOB process |

> **Watch-out — "Medicare Advantage is my secondary."** Patients often say this. **An MA plan is the primary**, not a secondary — it replaces Part B entirely, and there is no "Medicare secondary" for an MA patient. Correct this before discussing coverage.

For PMD orders requiring submission to both Medicare and an MA plan, see [[§5-6.2]].

## §10-8 Prior authorization

> **Short version.** The intake team's authorization members submit prior authorizations. Not every order needs one — it depends on the payer and the item. If the note says pending authorization, we are waiting on the payer and there is nothing more for the patient to send.

The standard order process is in [[§0-8]]; Power's differs — see [[§5-1.1]].

| Payer | When PA is required |
|---|---|
| Traditional Medicare | Only for a defined list: power wheelchairs, LALM (E0277), certain knee and back braces (L1832, L1833, L1851, L0648, L0650), lower-limb prosthetics |
| Medicare Advantage | Commonly for most DME, including beds, CPAP, oxygen |
| Commercial | Common for rentals and high-cost items; plan-specific |

Authorization **problems** — denied, expired, wrong item authorized — go to the department's manager or supervisor. Order **re-opens** go by email, never a transfer — see [[§0-12.3]].

> **Script — PA delay.** "I understand the wait can be frustrating. Your order is currently pending prior authorization from your insurance plan. We've submitted the request and are waiting for their approval before we can proceed with delivery. We'll contact you as soon as we hear back, and your insurance may also reach out."

## §10-9 Medicaid and Medicaid MCOs

> **Short version.** An MCO is simply the private insurer administering a patient's Medicaid — they **have** Medicaid. Medicare is billed first for dual eligibles, except a hospital bed where UMS is enrolled. Whether UMS is enrolled in that state decides the bed waiver.

UMS is contracted primarily with **Molina** for Medicaid managed care. Other MCOs and other states' Medicaid programs must be verified before an order proceeds. An MCO may require its own prior authorization even when Medicare is primary and has already paid.

> **Policy — enrollment governs beds, not rollators.** "Full Medicaid or Medicaid MCO in a state where UMS is enrolled" triggers the hospital bed upgrade-fee waiver and billing Medicaid as primary for the bed. Medicaid from a state we are **not** enrolled with still counts for the rollator waivers. See [[§10-15]].

Medicaid-specific coverage is in [[§0-7.1]].

## §10-10 Commercial insurance

> **Short version.** Network status is **plan-by-plan, never carrier-by-carrier**. Never say "we take UHC." Deductibles are larger than Medicare's, copays exist, and every plan has an out-of-pocket maximum.

The same carriers sell commercial, Medicare Advantage, and Medicaid MCO plans, so the carrier name alone doesn't tell you the coverage type. Look at the plan type on the card.

| Term | How it differs from traditional Medicare |
|---|---|
| Deductible | Often much larger. May follow a plan year, not a calendar year. Family deductibles exist |
| Coinsurance | A **percentage** of the allowed amount. Varies 10–50%; out-of-network is higher. Orders with a very high coinsurance (often 50% or more) may be declined, depending on the item — the intake team decides |
| Copay | A **flat dollar amount** per item or per rental month. Medicare has no copays for DME |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | Every commercial plan has one; traditional Medicare does not |
| In/out of network | OON claims pay less or nothing, and the patient may owe the difference. Medicare has no network |

**Other differences:** rental terms vary — do not assume the 13-month rule. Some plans may pay for an item in full, or as a one-time purchase, where Medicare would rent it. Same-or-similar periods vary. No ABN. If the patient also has Medicare, the MSP rules in [[§10-6.2]] decide which is primary.

> **Policy** — BCBS commercial as primary waives the **hospital bed** upgrade fees. It does **not** waive the rollator fee. See [[§10-15]].

> **Note — VGM Homelink.** Homelink is a national managed care and ancillary healthcare coordination company. It connects insurance payers and case managers with a network of independent healthcare providers, and UMS is one of those providers. A patient whose benefits are coordinated through Homelink may have an item covered that would otherwise be out of pocket — the knee walker, for example ([[§2-6]]). If a caller mentions Homelink or a case manager, check before quoting an out-of-pocket price.

> **Script — commercial cost question.** "Your plan works a little differently from Medicare. Your cost will depend on your plan's deductible, how much of it you've met this year, and your coinsurance or copay for medical equipment. Once we have your referral, our verification team will check your specific benefits and we'll contact you with an estimate before anything ships."

## §10-11 Hospice and the Certificate of Terminal Illness

> **Short version.** Medicare pays the hospice agency a daily rate covering equipment **related to the terminal illness**. Our claims for related equipment deny from the election date; equipment **unrelated** to the terminal diagnosis can still be billed to Part B. Billing decides which applies. **Don't tell a family Medicare will not cover it** — it is covered, but through the hospice instead of through us.

When a Medicare patient elects hospice, Medicare pays the hospice agency a daily rate covering all care related to the terminal illness, including DME. The hospice supplies that equipment through its own supplier.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Patient with an active UMS rental enters hospice | If the equipment relates to the terminal diagnosis, claims deny from the election date and rental billing stops — a pick-up is usually requested, **unless** ownership already transferred (month 14+ on a capped rental), in which case it stays. Unrelated equipment can continue under Part B. Billing decides which applies |
| New order for a patient already on hospice | Should come from the hospice agency. Families wanting UMS can do so only as an out-of-pocket purchase or rental |
| Hospice ends | Part B coverage resumes and a new referral can be processed |

**The CTI** is the document a hospice physician signs certifying a life expectancy of six months or less. It states the terminal diagnosis and the hospice election date. Billing may request it to confirm when hospice began and whether our item relates to the terminal diagnosis. If a provider's office calls "about the CTI," they are responding to that request — take a message or transfer to Billing.

**CSR actions:** note the hospice agency name and start date, route to Billing, and create a pick-up ticket only when Billing or the family requests it — Billing confirms first whether the equipment is patient-owned. Coordination between the hospice and Field Operations may be needed.

> **Script — family says the patient is now on hospice.** "Thank you for letting us know. When someone is on hospice, Medicare covers their equipment through the hospice agency rather than through a supplier like us, so their hospice nurse can usually provide what's needed. I'll note this on the account and have our billing team review the rental and let you know the next steps, including whether the equipment will need to be picked up."

## §10-12 Rental vs. purchase billing

{{diagram:rental-timeline}}

> **Short version.** Capped rental is **13 paid months, then ownership transfers automatically** — no purchase option (a complex rehab power wheelchair aside), no 15-month period. Oxygen is 36 months and the patient **never** owns it. The first invoice arrives 30–60 days after delivery.

### §10-12.1 Capped rental (13-month rule)

Medicare pays a monthly rental for 13 months of continuous use, after which ownership transfers automatically and Medicare stops paying rental.

- Months 1–3: 10% of the purchase fee-schedule amount. Months 4–13: 7.5%. (Power wheelchairs: 15% then 6%)
- The patient's share each month is the deductible if unmet, plus 20% coinsurance of that month's rental
- The patient signs the **PRF** at delivery, acknowledging the capped-rental billing — check for it before conceding a "nobody told me" call ([[§6-6.2]])
- During the rental the supplier owns the equipment and handles maintenance and repairs at no charge
- After transfer, Medicare covers medically necessary repairs and parts for the rest of the useful life
- The 13 months must be **continuous use** — interruptions can pause or reset the count, see [[§10-14]]
- The exception is a **complex rehab power wheelchair**, which can be billed as a purchase at delivery — see [[§5-9.3]]

### §10-12.2 Oxygen (36-month rule)

| Period | What happens |
|---|---|
| Months 1–36 | Medicare pays a monthly rental. The supplier owns the equipment and is responsible for all maintenance, supplies, and servicing |
| Month 37 onward | Medicare stops paying rental. **The supplier keeps ownership** but must continue to furnish equipment, supplies, and servicing for the remainder of the 5-year RUL. Repairs are never separately paid; for a concentrator or transfill equipment, Medicare pays **one maintenance-and-servicing visit every 6 months** from 6 months after the rental ends — a real visit, with the patient's 20% coinsurance. Tanks and liquid oxygen have no such visit |
| After the 5-year RUL | If oxygen is still needed, a new 36-month rental begins with replacement equipment |

> **Policy — the patient never owns oxygen equipment.** Do **not** tell an oxygen patient they will own the concentrator after 36 months. Say: "Medicare pays for the rental for 36 months. After that, you keep using the equipment at no rental charge and we continue to service it, for up to 5 years total."

**Accessories are included for the full 5 years.** Per CMS Policy Article A52514, oxygen accessories are included in the monthly rental allowance during the 36 months. After month 36 the supplier keeps providing them — with the equipment and its repairs — for the rest of the 5-year RUL, with no separate payment, and supplies any accessory the treating practitioner orders at no additional charge. Covered: cannulas (A4615), tubing (A4616), standard regulators (E1353), oxygen conserving devices (A9900) when ordered, humidifiers (E0555), and stands and racks (E1355). A patient should **not** see a separate bill for these at any point in the 5 years.

When a new 36-month period can begin is in [[§10-12.8]].

### §10-12.3 When a claim can be submitted

Insurance is billed after delivery, never before.

| Item type | Date of service | When the claim can go out |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase items | The delivery date, or shipping date for shipped items | On or after the DOS, once proof of delivery and the SWO are on file. One claim only |
| Rental items | The monthly anniversary date (same calendar day each month as delivery) | One claim per rental month, on or after that month's anniversary |
| Resupply items | The shipping date if sent by carrier; the delivery date if we deliver it | Patient confirms the refill within the 30 days before the current supply runs out; ship no sooner than 10 days before it runs out |
| Delivered to a hospital or SNF before discharge | The discharge date (delivery allowed up to 2 days before for fitting) | On or after discharge |

> **Policy — timely filing.** Traditional Medicare: 12 months from DOS. Texas Medicaid: 95 days. MA and commercial: by contract, commonly 90–180 days. **A claim filed late is denied and cannot be billed to the patient.** This is why missing documentation is chased urgently — the clock runs from delivery, not from when paperwork arrives. If a patient asks about a claim filed late, they won't be billed for it.

### §10-12.4 Why the first invoice arrives 30–60 days after delivery

Item delivered → claim submitted → payer processes (electronic Medicare claims typically about two weeks; commercial longer) → secondary processes → UMS receives the remittance showing patient responsibility → UMS invoices. For rentals this repeats monthly.

> **Policy** — Patients should not be asked to pay estimated deductible or coinsurance before the claim has processed; the amount isn't known until the remittance arrives. The exceptions are upgrade fees and out-of-pocket purchases, collected up front via Pre-Pay — [[§10-19.3]].

### §10-12.5 Same or Similar (SOS)

Medicare assigns most DME a 5-year reasonable useful lifetime. If the patient received the same or similar equipment — same HCPCS or category — within the past 5 years from **any** supplier, Medicare will not pay for a replacement unless the item was lost, stolen, or irreparably damaged — see [[§7-10.1]] for what counts. One of the most common denial reasons. The patient may purchase out of pocket, or an ABN can be issued — [[§10-17.1]]. For a PMD, an upgrade supported by the provider's attestation of a significant change in condition may be possible within the 5 years — [[§4-5.1]]. Commercial and Medicaid rules vary.

### §10-12.6 Standard Written Orders

An SWO is required before DME can be provided. It must include the patient's name or MBI, the order date, a description of the item or its HCPCS code, quantity if applicable, the prescribing practitioner's name and NPI, and their signature. Power wheelchairs additionally require a **face-to-face encounter and a written order before delivery** — by statute. CMS keeps a list of other items with the same requirement, updated by notice; it includes some hospital beds, such as the **bariatric bed (E0301)**, but not the semi-electric bed. **Every hospital bed**, including semi-electric, still needs **clinical notes from within the last 6 months** to be processed through insurance. Incomplete or missing SWOs are a top cause of Medicare denials — which is why an SWO or MRx is mandatory before an order proceeds, under insurance guidelines.

### §10-12.7 Geographic rate variation

Medicare-approved amounts vary by area, with different rates for rural and non-rural ZIP codes and former competitive bidding areas. Billing applies the fee schedule for the patient's delivery address.

> **Watch-out** — Because of this variation, do not quote specific dollar amounts unless verified. Use "your estimated cost based on Medicare's approved amount" — [[§10-20.2]].

### §10-12.8 Oxygen — when a new 36-month period begins

The most misunderstood area of oxygen billing.

**A short break in need.** If oxygen use ends for **less than 60 days plus the remainder of the rental month of discontinuation** and then resumes, payment resumes where it left off. No new rental period, no new qualifying test, no new documentation.

> **Policy — the calculation includes the rest of the rental month.** The threshold is 60 days **plus** the days left in the **rental month** when use stopped. A rental month runs from the monthly anniversary of delivery, not the calendar month.
>
> Example: delivered on the 15th, so each rental month runs from the 15th to the 14th. Oxygen stops on 10 March, leaving 4 days of that rental month (11–14 March). The threshold is 60 + 4 = **64 days**. Resuming on or before 13 May picks up where it left off; resuming on 14 May or later starts a new period.

**A long break, during months 1–36.** If use ends for **more than** that threshold and new medical necessity is established, a **new 36-month period begins**, requiring:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| New prescription | Detailed written order; WOPD if required |
| New qualifying blood gas study | ABG or pulse oximetry within 30 days prior to the new initial date |
| Face-to-face evaluation | Within 30 days of the new initial certification |
| Documentation of new medical need | Clinical notes supporting why oxygen is needed again |
| Documentation of the interruption | Cause, length, pick-up date of the old equipment, new delivery date |
| Claim narrative | First month's claim must state "Break in medical need greater than 60 days" |

**A break during months 37–60.** A new rental period does **not** begin. The supplier who provided oxygen during the 36th rental month must continue providing all necessary items and services for the remainder of the 5-year RUL.

**What restarts the 36 months**

| Situation | New 36-month period? | New qualifying test? |
|---|---|---|
| Break ≤ 60 days plus remainder of month | No — resumes | No |
| Break > 60 days during months 1–36 | **Yes** | **Yes** — within 30 days of the new start |
| Break > 60 days during months 37–60 | No — original supplier continues | No |
| Equipment reaches 5-year RUL and the patient elects new equipment | **Yes** | No — most recent qualifying test accepted |
| Equipment lost, stolen, or irreparably damaged — see [[§7-10.1]] for what counts | **Yes** | No — most recent test accepted |
| Supplier abandons the patient, or Chapter 7 bankruptcy | **Yes** | No — most recent test accepted |
| Hospital or SNF stay, patient still needs oxygen | No — resumes | No |
| Switch from Medicare Advantage to Fee-for-Service | No — resumes, with documentation | No — most recent test accepted |
| Change in equipment modality or supplier | No | No |
| Malfunction, wear and tear, or routine repair | No | No |

> **Note — MA to FFS.** A new blood gas study is not required. The most recent qualifying study the patient previously obtained is used, per DME MAC policy.

**The 5-year RUL.** The reasonable useful lifetime is **5 years from the initial date of service** — not the age of the equipment. After it expires the patient may elect new equipment, starting a new 36-month period. The 5 years does **not** restart for modality changes, equipment swap-outs, or supplier changes.

> **Policy — replacement is all or nothing.** If the patient elects replacement after the RUL, **both stationary and portable equipment must be replaced at the same time**. A new 36-month period and a new RUL begin for both. The first month's claim needs the RA modifier, but no new qualifying blood gas testing.

## §10-13 Compliance — the billing view

> **Short version.** Coverage for four categories continues only while the patient demonstrates ongoing need. PAP is **usage-based**; oxygen, ventilator and LALM are **documentation-based**. Answer from the compliance specialists' notes; transfer when they don't settle it.

What each item requires, and what happens when it isn't met, is one table: [[§3-12.2]]. The stages and the grace period are in [[§3-12.1]]. Resupply for PAP patients also requires documented ongoing use; a patient out of compliance cannot receive supplies until it is resolved.

## §10-14 Rental lifecycle events

> **Short version.** A hospital or SNF stay **pauses** the rental count; it does not cancel it, and the equipment stays home. Rental months follow the patient, not the supplier. The pick-up ticket is what ends a rental.

| Event | Billing effect | CSR action |
|---|---|---|
| Admitted to hospital or SNF | Medicare will not pay a rental month whose billing date falls during the stay. The count pauses and resumes after discharge. **Equipment stays in the home** | Note the admission and expected discharge date. Do not create a pick-up ticket for a short stay |
| Break in medical need of **60 days plus the rest of that rental month**, or less | The original rental period continues from where it left off | Route the new order normally |
| Break in medical need **longer** than 60 days plus the rest of that rental month | A new 13-month rental period starts, with a new SWO. Deductible and coinsurance restart from month 1. The same threshold as oxygen — worked example in [[§10-12.8]] | Set the patient's expectation that billing restarts |
| Patient passes away | Billing stops as of the date of death. A final invoice for prior months may still be owed by the estate | Express condolences and note the date. Patient-owned equipment stays with the family; otherwise create a pick-up ticket. See [[§1-5]] |
| Moves within service area | No billing impact; the fee schedule may change if the ZIP moves between rural and non-rural | Update the address; notify Field Ops if re-delivery is needed |
| Moves out of state or out of service area | Rental continues only if UMS can service the new address. **Medicaid is state-specific and does not travel.** | Confirm whether UMS services it. If not, create a pick-up ticket and advise a referral to a local supplier |
| Switches to UMS mid-rental | Medicare tracks rental months **per patient, not per supplier**. UMS receives only the months remaining | Ask which supplier and roughly when the rental started |
| Switches from UMS | Rental stops on the pick-up date. Remaining months belong to the new supplier | Create a pick-up ticket |
| Insurance changes mid-rental | The new payer may require its own PA and may treat rental months differently | Collect the new insurance and effective date, route to verification before the next billing date |
| No longer needs the item | Rental stops on the pick-up date. Coinsurance already paid is not refunded | Create a pick-up ticket |
| Breaks during rental | Supplier-owned; repaired or swapped at no charge | Create a service/swap ticket. Do not quote repair charges |
| Breaks after ownership transferred (month 14+) | Patient-owned. Medicare covers necessary repairs and parts | Route as a repair order. If replacement is requested within 5 years, explain SOS |

> **Policy — pick-up tickets.** The pick-up ticket is the trigger to stop billing — Billing uses the pick-up date to end the rental, so accuracy matters. How to create one: [[§6-9.3]].

### §10-14.1 Pick-up write-offs

Not every pick-up ticket ends with the equipment collected. **Field Ops decides** which outcome applies, sends the write-off notice and contacts the patient.

| Outcome | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Waived** — a company decision that the item isn't worth recovering | The remaining balance is waived and the item becomes the patient's |
| **Failed pick-up** — no response, or the patient refuses | The balance stays on the account. The patient is still liable and may return the item later; we can't force payment |

Never promise a caller either outcome — see [[§6-9.1]] and, for power chairs, [[§5-8.1]].

## §10-15 Upgrade fees and the waiver matrix

> **Short version.** There are exactly **three** waiver rules. If a situation is not one of them, the fee is charged. Patient lift fees are never waived, and CSRs cannot waive a fee outside these rules.

An upgrade fee covers features insurance considers beyond medical necessity — a seat and brakes on a rollator, electric height adjustment on a bed, a motor on a lift. Upgrade fees are **not covered by any secondary insurance or assistance program**, and the patient must agree before the order proceeds.

> **Policy — the three traps.**
>
> 1. **QMB-only is not full Medicaid.** Standard rollator fee only. The heavy-duty rollator fee is still charged, and QMB-only does not qualify for Rule B.
> 2. **Enrollment matters for beds, not rollators.** Rule A applies whether or not UMS is enrolled in the patient's Medicaid state. Rule B requires enrollment.

### §10-15.3 Call handling

A returned call about an upgrade fee is usually to inform the patient and confirm they'll proceed. **Accepted:** note that the fee is acceptable. **Declined:** reiterate that the fee is required for the upgraded item, offer the standard item at no upgrade fee, and email to close the order or change the item.

## §10-17 ABN, EOBs, denials and appeals

> **Short version.** An EOB or MSN is **not a bill** — the patient owes the patient-responsibility line, never the billed charge. The ABN is Medicare-only and must be signed before delivery. Never tell a patient a denial will be overturned.

### §10-17.1 Advance Beneficiary Notice

Given to a traditional Medicare patient **before** an item is provided, when Medicare may not cover it — most often a same-or-similar situation, a documentation gap, or an item outside coverage criteria.

- The patient signs and selects an option: receive the item and accept potential responsibility with the claim still submitted, receive it without a claim, or decline
- **If no ABN is signed and Medicare denies for medical-necessity reasons, UMS generally cannot bill the patient**
- Not used for Medicare Advantage or commercial plans
- Not a blanket form — it must describe the specific item and the specific reason

### §10-17.2 EOBs and MSNs

Both show, per claim: amount billed, amount allowed, amount paid, and patient responsibility. **Neither is a bill.** The billed amount will look much higher than the allowed amount; that is normal.

> **Script — "my EOB says you charged $900 for a walker."** "The amount on that line is what we submit to insurance, which is a standard billed charge. The number that matters is the 'allowed' or 'approved' amount — that's what insurance actually recognizes for the item — and then 'patient responsibility' is your share of that. Any bill you receive from us will be based only on that patient responsibility amount, never the billed charge."

### §10-17.3 Denials and appeals

The Denials team reviews the reason and either corrects and resubmits or determines the denial stands. Common reasons: same-or-similar, missing or incomplete SWO or face-to-face notes, no prior authorization, COB, and hospice enrollment.

For traditional Medicare, the first appeal level (redetermination) must be requested **within 120 days** of the MSN date. MA and commercial deadlines are printed on the EOB.

Explain that the denial is under review and route to Denials. **Don't tell the patient the denial will be overturned.** If they want the item regardless, offer the out-of-pocket option.

Two common calls go straight to the Denials team member working the case — the recent notes name them:

| Caller | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Insurance**, calling in response to a Denials request — an authorization request, a resubmission or similar | Call the Denials team member working the case. If they're not available, the Denials Q (ext 101) — `[ROLE: Denials Team]` |
| **The patient**, calling about an invoice a Denials team member sent, the billing status or similar, while a Denials team member is actively working it | The same — the team member working the case, otherwise the Denials Q (ext 101) |

## §10-18 Resupply billing

> **Short version.** Quantities are in [[§3-3.2]]. What matters here is the timing rule and the compliance gate.

> **Policy — shipping windows.** The patient confirms the refill **within the 30 days** before the current supply runs out, and we ship no sooner than **10 days** before it runs out.

Shipping more than the allowance, or sooner than these windows allow, is **overutilization** — insurance denies the excess. A PAP patient out of compliance can't receive supplies until it's resolved — [[§10-13]].

Medicare's replacement frequencies are the default. Texas Medicaid sets its own limits for incontinence supplies, and other payers differ. Always confirm against the Item Master and the verification note before promising a quantity or a date.

PAP supply allowances are in [[§3-7.3]]; nebulizer supply allowances are in [[§3-3.3]]; the three-month shipping limit is in [[§3-3.2]].

### §10-19.6 When a patient can't pay the full amount

Two options exist for a patient who cannot pay the full amount, and both let them keep the equipment rather than surrender it:

| Option | Set up by |
|---|---|
| **Economic Assistance Application (EAA)** | Processed by **Billing**. A CSR or Billing sends the patient the blank form — see below |
| **Payment plan** | The **Billing team** — transfer or route the request to them |

> **Policy — out-of-pocket orders qualify for neither.** A patient without insurance can still purchase equipment from us, but an **OOP order is ineligible for the EAA and for a payment plan**. Don't offer either on an out-of-pocket purchase.

**How the EAA works**

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | A CSR or the Billing team sends the patient the **blank EAA form** — it's in the CSR shared drive, or in the UMS App under `Admin` → `Service Provider Forms` |
| 2 | The patient completes it — monthly income, expenses and related financial information — and returns it to us |
| 3 | The completed form goes to **Billing**, who process it |
| Turnaround | No set timeframe — usually **within 1–2 weeks** |

## §10-20 Patient communication

> **Short version.** Swap the jargon for plain language. Never promise coverage, approval, a delivery date, or a fee waiver — describe the process instead of the outcome.

### §10-20.1 Language substitutions

| ✗ Instead of | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Coinsurance | "Your share of the cost" |
| Deductible not met | "You haven't yet reached the annual amount you pay before insurance starts covering part of the cost" |
| Capped rental | "Your insurance rents this equipment for about 13 months, then you own it" |
| Prior authorization | "Your insurance requires us to get approval before we can deliver the equipment" |
| SOS issue | "Our records show insurance covered similar equipment recently, so they won't cover a new one right now" |
| Compliance (oxygen, vent, LALM) | "Your doctor needs to provide updated paperwork so insurance will continue paying for your equipment" |
| Compliance (CPAP/BiPAP) | "Insurance needs to see that you're using the machine regularly during the first 90 days, and that you've followed up with your doctor" |
| ABN | "A form that lets you know Medicare might not cover this, so you can decide if you'd like to proceed" |
| EOB / MSN | "The statement your insurance sends explaining what they paid — it's not a bill" |
| QMB | "A program through Medicaid that covers your Medicare costs, so you shouldn't be billed for them" |
| COB | "Your insurance needs you to confirm which plan should pay first" |
| MCO | "The insurance company that manages your Medicaid plan" |
| HCPCS code | "The billing code for your equipment" |
| Out of network | "Your plan doesn't have a contract with us, so it may pay less or not at all" |

### §10-20.2 What you can and cannot promise

| ✓ You can say | ✗ You should not say |
|---|---|
| "Based on your coverage, your estimated cost would be…" | "Your insurance will definitely cover this" |
| "We'll submit this to your insurance and follow up" | "This will be approved" |
| "Medicare typically covers 80% after the deductible" | "You'll only owe $X" |
| "I can check on the status of your order" | "Your equipment will arrive by [date]" |
| "Let me connect you with our billing team" | "I can waive that fee" |
| "Our verification team will confirm whether we're in-network with your specific plan" | "We take UHC / Aetna / BCBS" |
| "Medicare pays for the oxygen rental for 36 months, and we keep servicing it after that" | "You'll own the oxygen concentrator after 36 months" |

## §10-21 FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How much will I owe? | Depends on coverage and whether the annual deductible is met. For traditional Medicare, after the deductible the patient owes 20% of the approved amount. Medigap or Medicaid may cover that 20% |
| Why am I being billed? I thought Medicare covers this. | Medicare covers it, but there is a patient responsibility portion — 20% after the deductible |
| Do you accept my insurance? | Depends on the specific plan, not the carrier name. Have the referral sent; verification confirms |
| Can I just buy this out of pocket? | Yes. No paperwork needed since we're not billing insurance — except oxygen concentrators, PAP devices and ventilators, which need a prescription regardless |
| Why does my rollator have an extra fee? | A rollator has a seat, basket, and hand brakes over a standard walker. **Any active Medicaid plan waives it** — except that QMB-only waives the standard rollator fee only, not the heavy-duty. See [[§10-15]] |
| How long until I own my equipment? | A purchased item is yours on delivery. Most rentals: about 13 months. Oxygen is different: Medicare pays 36 months, then no rental charge while we keep servicing it — the patient does not own it. Ventilators are never owned |
| What happens if I'm out of compliance? | Depends on the equipment. Oxygen, vent, LALM: updated doctor paperwork. CPAP: usage during the first 90 days plus the follow-up visit |
| Can my secondary cover what my primary doesn't? | Usually it covers the remaining share on items the primary approves, not the full cost of an item the primary denies. **Texas Medicaid is the exception** for items Medicare never covers, such as incontinence supplies and shower chairs. See [[§10-5.3]] |
| I have a QMB card — why did I get a bill? | They shouldn't have. Route to Billing immediately to correct and refund |
| My insurance needs coordination of benefits information. | Only the patient can update it. Advise them to call the number on the card, then call back once it's updated so we can resubmit |
| I got a statement saying you charged $900 for a walker. | That's an EOB, not a bill. See [[§10-17.2]] |
| My mother just started hospice — what happens to her bed? | Medicare pays the hospice agency for equipment related to her terminal diagnosis. Billing reviews whether the bed is related and whether it will be picked up; if she already owns it, it stays |
| I'm moving to another state — can I take my rental? | If we can service the new address, yes. Rental months carry over |
| I was in the hospital last month. | Medicare doesn't pay the rental for that period and we pause billing. It resumes after discharge |
| I got my walker two weeks ago and haven't received a bill. | Normal. We bill insurance after delivery and invoice once they've processed — usually a month or more |
| I had a bed from another company. Can I switch? | Yes. Medicare tracks rental months per patient, so we may be able to resume where they left off |

## §10-22 Compliance and privacy

Verification before discussing any account is in [[§1-3]]. For Medicare-assigned claims, UMS cannot bill patients more than the Medicare-approved deductible and coinsurance — upgrade fees, agreed in advance, are the exception, and QMB patients owe no Medicare cost-sharing at all ([[§10-5.3]]).

> **Critical — anti-kickback.** Never offer or provide anything of value to patients, physicians, or referral sources in exchange for referrals or orders. This includes gifts, discounts not available to all patients, waiving cost-sharing outside the documented rules, and special arrangements.

**When in doubt, escalate.** Take a message and have the appropriate team follow up rather than guess. Incorrect billing information creates financial and legal problems for the patient and the company.
